**The bricks of the spreading of PME are a buffer of their own, which the copy into the grid leaves zero.** The copy of the bricks into the grid of the transform (D85) ran a thread for each point of the grid and read the bricks 64 bytes apart, eight sectors for one, and a kernel cleared the bricks before each spreading: 61 and 28 µs a step on Cellulose. The bricks now have a buffer of their own (f32, 16 values for each z of each brick of 4 × 4 points; the grid of fixed point no longer lends its room), zeroed once when it is allocated, and the copy is a transpose through the memory of the block: a block takes a brick and 32 of its slabs along z, reads the 512 values in order into a tile with rows of 17 (no bank conflicts), writes zeros back after the reads, and a warp writes each of the 16 rows of the brick as 32 values along z in order. Zeros written beside the reads held the reads behind them (the compiler cannot tell them apart): 204 µs; written after the barrier, 63 µs for the copy and the clearing together, from 89. Cellulose NVE: 3039 µs a step of the device, from 3073 (RTX 3090); the reciprocal energy agrees to the rounding of the atomics.
