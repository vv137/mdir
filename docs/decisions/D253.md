# D253

Recorded 2026-10-13.

**A simulation keeps the renumbered members of its tuples and their incidence structures from one activation of its entry to the next; a start takes them only where the identities of the particles and the members are equal, element for element, to those they were built from, and builds them in the memory of the last where they are not.** Issue #294, amends D215, under which an activation that ended freed everything it had built. *Measured first* (`main` f378ac4; `scripts/benchmarks/activation/start.py`; the three explicit systems of the Amber suite with PME, flexible, the length of a soft pair term tunable). A program of a simulation orders its particles once, where an activation begins; there every tuple set (five on these systems) is renumbered on the host and its incidence structure built there in new memory, a dense matrix of one row a particle as wide as the particle with the most tuples needs: 42.6 MB on JAC, 164.4 MB on Factor IX, 696.3 MB on Cellulose, 1.7 to 1.8 kB an atom on all three, and on a device uploaded. The host's part, the same loops on both targets, took 2 ms, 14 ms, and 315 ms on the CPU target: 0.09, 0.15, and 0.77 µs an atom. The part that grows faster than the system is the memory, not the algorithm: the build writes little (8 million numbers for the dihedrals of Cellulose), but the C library recycles freed blocks only up to 32 MiB, so that a larger structure is mapped from the system at every start, faulted in page by page, and unmapped at its free. On JAC no structure is over that size, on Factor IX one, on Cellulose four (669 of 696 MB); the build loop alone at the size of Cellulose's dihedrals takes 190 to 205 ms in new memory with 35 to 44 ms for its free (86,600 page faults) and 16 ms in memory that is kept. On a GPU (one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, mixed precision, `main` with marks around each part) a start with its evaluation took 5.04 ms on JAC, 61.9 ms on Factor IX, and 264 ms on Cellulose, of which the tuples 2.97, 55.7, and 233 ms: the reads of the identities, one for each set, 0.08, 0.20, and 0.70 ms; the renumbering 0.12, 0.64, and 4.4 ms; the build 0.79, 38.3, and 167 ms; the upload of the structures 1.88, 9.5, and 40.4 ms; that of the members 0.08, 0.19, and 1.3 ms; the free of the structures of the host 0.03, 6.9, and 19.2 ms. The rest on Cellulose was the end of the last activation and the state (7.9 ms), the arguments of the entry (12.8 ms), the uploads of the model with the order (4.0 ms), and the evaluation (6.4 ms). *The store.* The lowerings of a program of an embedding front end (`Control::segments`: a Python simulation and the simulation of a frame evaluator) take the renumbered members of a tuple set, its incidence structure, and on a GPU outside the deterministic mode the copy of the members for the loops that evaluate each tuple once, from functions of the runtime, and give them back where they would free them (the option `kept-tuples` of `convert-md-exec-to-loops` and `convert-md-exec-to-gpu`; `runtime/mdrt_tuples.h`, with the host's functions in `mdrt.c` and the device's in `mdrt_cuda.c`). The simulation opens a store with its first activation, tells it where each activation begins, and closes it at its end. Both structures are functions of the members in the order of the files and of the identity of the particle at each place, and of nothing else, so the store keeps a copy of these two inputs with each structure and compares the inputs that the code gives, element for element: nothing is inferred from what the host did since the last start, no call of the simulation marks anything valid, and a kept structure that is taken is the one that would be built. What invalidates one is therefore exactly a change of the order (after steps; after a commit of a borrow that moves a particle to another cell of the order; a simulation that continues a checkpoint has a store of its own) or of the members; an update of the tunables, an evaluation, `gradient()`, a commit of velocities, parameters, or a cell, and a checkpoint invalidate nothing. With `MDRT_CHECK_KEPT_TUPLES` in the environment every structure that is taken is also built anew and compared, on a device after reading it back, and a difference ends the program (the maintainer's ruling on PR #315: the variable may exist, as a diagnostic of the kind of `MDRT_WAIT`). A structure that is not found is built in the memory of an entry of the same tuple set that the code of the activation does not hold, whose sizes do not depend on the order: one entry for each tuple set, whatever the number of starts and of orders, and no allocation at a start in a new order. The rows are written particle by particle from an index of the tuples, in the order of the tuples as before, and on a device at most 16 MiB at a time, in memory of the store taken once, each range uploaded before the next is written, so that the host never holds a whole structure of a device. New on the host: two copies of the members and one of the identities for each tuple set, 1.9 MB on JAC, 6.3 MB on Factor IX, 48 MB on Cellulose, and on a GPU those 16 MiB; the device holds what an activation held. The comparison costs 0.08 ms, 0.24 ms, and 1.7 ms on the three systems (CPU target, the five sets together). *Measured after* (one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, mixed precision, `main` and this decision on the same device; a start with its evaluation, ms, the least of five): where the particles have not moved (`gradient()` again), 4.95 to 2.01 on JAC, 60.9 to 6.08 on Factor IX, 413 to 29.5 on Cellulose (`main` on Cellulose gave 264 to 453 ms over four runs), with 0.13, 0.43, and 2.1 ms in the functions of the store; an update of the tunables with its evaluation, 14.9 to 8.29, 135 to 29.7, and 867 to 203, of which the store is 0.26, 0.78, and 4.2 ms, so that what an update costs beyond its two starts is now most of it (#324); in a new order (20 steps and `gradient()`), 4.98 to 5.08 on JAC, where no structure was over 32 MiB and nothing is gained, 57.5 to 17.3 on Factor IX, and 387 to 98.0 on Cellulose, of which the build with its uploads is 3.2, 12.4, and 75 ms. A step is not slower: runs of 20,000 steps in one part, `main` and this decision in turn six times in one job on one device, the five rounds after the first, take 1.5424 ms a step (standard deviation 0.0040) on `main` and 1.5444 (0.0035) here on Cellulose, 0.13% more, and 0.3202 (0.0006) against 0.3210 (0.0006) on Factor IX, 0.24% more, both of the size of the spread; on JAC 0.1003 and 0.1007 against 0.1005 and 0.1007. The device in use holds the same memory under both (3,760 MiB on Cellulose, 1,294 MiB on Factor IX). The first run of a job, on a cold device, is 2% faster than the later ones under its cap of 300 W (1.5058 against 1.5424 on `main`), which is what earlier single runs in separate jobs had shown as a difference of 3.8% between the builds. The code of a step is not changed. *Not changed.* The builder; the emitted text of every program; the pipeline and the lowered module of `mdir run`, identical to the byte for JAC of the Amber suite; every kernel. The pipeline of a program of an embedding front end names the option, so its kept code (D236) and its entries of the compile cache are made anew once. The segments of `mdir run`, which begin in a new order each time, would gain from the kept memory alone (#316); the structures built on the device, for the starts in a new order, are #295. *Rejected:* telling the store from the simulation when its structures are valid (a path that changes the order and forgets to say so would be wrong in silence, where a comparison of 0.1 to 2 ms cannot be); keying on the order alone (the members given are compared as well, so that no assumption about what an update may change is built in); keeping the structures in the records of the activation and carrying them over (an activation ends by freeing its records, D215). *Validated.* The store called as the code calls it, against a renumbering and an incidence structure written in NumPy, with one identity and with one member changed; a kept structure written over by the test, which ends the program under the check and names the structure; a sequence of starts on the dipeptide in water (evaluations, an update of the tunables, 400 steps that order the particles anew, a commit of a borrow, a checkpoint, a run continued from it, and on the CPU a commit that moves a particle by more than a cell of the order), in the deterministic mode, in double and mixed precision, with the check on: every state, force, energy, and derivative equal to the bit to those of a simulation that builds every structure anew, with the numbers of structures taken and built at each start as they must be, and, as 156 records on the CPU and 124 on a GPU (RTX PRO 6000), equal to the bit to those of `main`; outside the deterministic mode on a GPU, the copies of the members taken with their structures under the check; the GPU sequence under compute-sanitizer, `memcheck` and `initcheck`, 0 errors each (61 s and 57 s on the device); two simulations and a frame evaluator of one program, which keep their structures apart (`python-kept-tuples*.test`, `kept-tuples.mlir` of both lowerings).
