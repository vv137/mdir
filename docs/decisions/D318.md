# D318

Recorded 2026-10-22.

**A short, static sum in a batched neural stage body can opt into one GPU thread per result, accumulating its 2 to 31 terms in a register in source order and fusing an eligible adjacent pointwise operation and duplicate result write; the loop and separate pointwise launches remain the default.** `md-exec-lower-stage-bodies{short-reductions=true}` or `MDIR_STAGE_SHORT_REDUCTIONS=registers` selects the combined strategy with `fuse=true` (the pass default). #477, part of #462 step 4b. The short and D303 warp strategies share the `RowSum` matcher, term emitter, and reduction combiner. The matcher accepts one f32/f64 reduction loop, a term independent of the accumulator, and read-only loads; writes, unknown effects, and nested regions fall back or fail the stage's purity verifier. An adjacent pointwise epilogue must have one input from the sum, identity maps, equal shape, and a fresh output; the sum remains stored for its other users. A duplicate final result writes to both caller buffers only when all input reads are from local allocations and the body has only `md.stage` symbol users, whose generated calls allocate distinct result buffers; otherwise the copies remain. Long contiguous sums still use a warp. There are no control-file keys or artifact changes; six cuBLAS calls, tile buffers, stage boundaries, and transfer dependencies stay explicit. *Measured*: on an RTX 4090, `node.b` over 4,096 rows takes 0.287998 ms against 0.313950 ms with loops, with 17 against 21 launches and six GEMM calls in both. In two alternated 20-step pairs on 6,144 atoms (mixed), the default took 482.504 and 482.345 ms/step and the combined strategy 475.182 and 475.279 ms/step; peak allocation fell from 621.5 to 613.5 MiB. The default exported stage IR is byte-identical to main. This roughly 1.5% whole-step gain on one device and model supports retaining the opt-in, without making it default.
