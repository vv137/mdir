# D251

Recorded 2026-10-12.

**The frames of a frame evaluator are evaluated inside one activation of a program of frames, whose entry is a loop over frames that takes the positions and the cell of each at a boundary; the program keeps the particles in the order of the input, and the energy of a frame is a function of the frame alone.** Issue #291, amends D240 and D243. D240 began an activation at every frame (D215): of a frame of JAC on one RTX 4090 in mixed precision, 5.0 ms, the kernels with the neighbor build are 0.8 ms; the rest is the copy of the model to the device and, above all, the tuples renumbered on the host and uploaded with their incidence structures (43 MB on JAC). *The program.* `Control::frames`, set by the simulation of an evaluator as `dependentTerms` is and by no key of a control file, makes the entry of the program of segments an endless `scf.for` in place of the start and the loop over parts. Everything but the positions and the cell is taken once, before the loop. An iteration calls `@mdrtFrameBoundary`, where the entry waits on its stack as at `@mdrtPartBoundary`; the call returns the buffer of the host with the positions of the next frame and writes the six numbers of its cell (triclinic included, D238). It then places the virtual sites and runs the text of the evaluation at the start of a run, unchanged, in a loop of one iteration with `mdrt.segment`, so that the neighbor structures are built anew, in storage allocated once; the results reach the host through the calls of a start, and the host applies the chain rule of D230 at each frame as before. The buffer of the positions is a value of the iteration, the result of a call, so that no pass takes one frame for another; `md-exec-assign-storage` gives a `mdrt.from_buffer` of a buffer that a call in the body of a loop returns a buffer of the loop and a copy, since a buffer that a loop owns may hold what the loop carries. The rows that the evaluation writes on the stack are taken before the loop: nothing on the device or the stack grows with the frames (300,000 frames in one activation with the resident memory constant), and there is no limit on $K$. *The order* (ruled on PR #293). Three were measured: the spatial order chosen at every frame, 4.41 ms a frame on JAC and 55.6 ms on Factor IX, since every frame renumbers the tuples; the order of the input, 0.69 ms and 1.95 ms; and the spatial order of the first frame of an activation, 0.69 ms and 1.96 ms. The kernels take the same time in any of them (Factor IX: 1.849 ms against 1.868 ms with `MDRT_WAIT`), because the neighbor structures bin the particles themselves, and the order of the input of JAC and Factor IX is already as far from local as a shuffle of their waters. The order of the input was taken, whatever `Execution.reorder` of the program says: it alone leaves the energy of a frame a function of the frame, the program, and the values, with the same bits in any call and after any frames. *What changes in the numbers.* Against a sampler compiled in the order of the input the evaluator agrees to the bit; against one in the spatial order, the default, within the rounding of sums in another order: $1.7\times10^{-10}$ kJ/mol in double precision and $1.8\times10^{-4}$ to $3.2\times10^{-4}$ kJ/mol in mixed precision on systems of about 1,200 atoms, $1.2\times10^{-4}$ kJ/mol of 307,567 on JAC. A reweighting takes both energies from the evaluator and is not affected. *The neighbor structure is not kept* from one frame to the next: stored frames are not within half a skin of each other, a kept structure would make the bits of a frame depend on the frames before it, and the build is 0.17 of 0.69 ms on JAC. *Failures.* A frame that is refused changes nothing; one whose evaluation fails ends the activation and restores the cell, the error names its index, nothing of the call is returned, and the next frame begins an activation. The activation lives across batches and calls and ends at an update of the tunables. *Interface.* Unchanged. `FrameEvaluator(program)` with `terms="all"` now compiles a program of its own, once for a `Program` (D236; 1.9 s on the dipeptide, 2.7 s on JAC, then 0.07 s), and the program of the dependent terms is kept with the program it is made from (1.6 s, then 0.05 s, where every evaluator paid 1.7 s). *Measured* (one RTX 4090, mixed precision, ms per frame, any $K$ from 4 on): the dipeptide in water 0.84 to 0.28 (the whole potential) and 0.43 to 0.16 (the dependent terms); JAC 5.00 to 0.69 and 2.07 to 0.45, 5.6 and 3.7 steps; a frame of JAC costs what its kernels cost (0.77 ms with a wait after every launch). A run is not slower: its program has the same text to the byte, and JAC of the Amber suite runs at 1306 and 1313 ns/day against 1303 and 1306. *Validated* against the evaluator of before on 26 stored frames of four sets (orthorhombic, under a barostat with a cell each, triclinic with its tilts, the dependent terms), each set in one call, one frame per call, reversed, with repeats and jumps, and after a failed frame: every output to the bit on the CPU and a GPU, in double and mixed precision, in the deterministic mode, with programs in the order of the input (`scripts/validation/frames/activation.py`); `python-frames-activation*.test`. *Not taken:* a relation over the particles of the types of a tunable Lennard-Jones pair (at most 0.25 of 0.73 ms a frame on JAC); the fields accumulated on the device with the chain rule applied once (the chain rule is not what a frame costs); frames read from a device tensor without a copy to the host (the copy is 0.03 ms on JAC). The tuples renumbered on the device are #295, with #294. *Amended (#309): the rows that the evaluation writes on the stack are no longer taken before the loop by the builder; they stand in the loop, where the start of a run takes them, and the lowerings keep the stack of the entry.* The builder had moved the lines that hold ` = memref.alloca() : ` before the loop and, since #303, refused a program of frames whose loop held an allocation in any other spelling. Since #305 `hoist-static-allocas` takes every allocation of a constant size once where the lowering for the CPU ends (D117), and the lowering for a device gives back the stack of each iteration of a loop of the host, so the matching of text was a second guard of what the lowerings do for every spelling. It is removed with the stack part of the check; the check of the arguments of the entry that a frame replaces and of an empty evaluation stays. For an allocation whose size is not a constant, which the pass does not move and which no program emits, the lowering of a program of frames for the CPU refuses the module (`refuse-rest`, D117). The emitted text of a program of frames differs by these lines alone, moved into the loop (the dipeptide with the whole potential: `%terms`, `%obstart`, `%tg_out`); the text of every other program is that of before, byte for byte. The key of the kept code of an evaluator changes with its text, in memory and on disk, on both targets: it is compiled anew once. Every output is that of before to the bit (the energy, the virial, the volume, the observed columns, and the Jacobian of the whole potential and of the dependent terms, on the CPU and a GPU, in double and mixed precision), and the depth of the stack at the boundary is the same after frame 201 as after frame 1, to the byte: that of before on the CPU, and on a GPU less by the rows, which are now given back at the end of a frame (`python-frames-loop*.test`).
