# D316

Recorded 2026-10-22.

**The most neighbors of a particle at the start, from which the driver sizes the rows of a neighbor structure without `neighbor_capacity` (D227), are counted exactly by cells, over the threads of the process, in place of the serial search over particles; the count is the same integer, so the width of the lowered program does not change.** #471, step 4 (the setup). *What*: `driver::countMostNeighbors` (`lib/Driver/NeighborCount.cpp`) bins the particles into cells as wide as the reach by a counting sort (offsets and one array of the positions as given, in the order of the cells), takes the distinct cells among the 27 around a cell once for all its members, and sweeps each member over contiguous candidates; the cells are taken in chunks by as many threads as the process may run on (`llvm::hardware_concurrency`, its CPU affinity), at most `MDIR_COMPILE_THREADS` where set, one below 16,384 particles. The sweep is also compiled for AVX2 without FMA and chosen at run time; the file is compiled with `-fno-trapping-math`, which lets the comparisons be vectorized and changes no value. *Why it is exact*: the candidates are those of the serial search (the same wrapping $x - L\lfloor x/L\rfloor$ and rounding of the cell index, $\max(1, \lfloor L/r\rfloor)$ cells along an axis, so the same coincidences with 1 or 2 cells, and the diagonal alone of a triclinic cell), and the test of a pair is the same operations on the positions as given: $d = x_i - x_j$, $d \leftarrow d - L\,\mathrm{round}(d/L)$ per axis, $r^2$ summed in the order $x, y, z$, $r^2 < r_\text{reach}^2$. $\mathrm{round}$ is computed as $t = \mathrm{trunc}(q)$ plus a step of $\pm 1$ where $q - t$ (exact) is at least $\tfrac12$ in magnitude, which equals `std::round` except for the sign of a zero, which the pair cannot see: $d - L\cdot(\pm 0) = d$ for $d \ne 0$, and $d^2 = +0$ for $d = \pm 0$. The particle itself is among the candidates of its cell once and its own test is subtracted. A count is a sum of integers and the result their maximum, so the order of the candidates and of the threads does not matter. *Alternatives*: an estimate from the density (rejected: the width is part of the lowered program, and the maintainer required the same integer for every input); the device's first build (the width is needed before the program is built); skipping the count where the width is unused (not proven byte-identical; not needed at about 3 s at ×11). *Validation*: `mdir-neighbor-count-test` keeps the serial search as the reference; equal on 16 made inputs with 1, 2, 3, 8 threads and the default, on the four systems of `neighbor-count.test`, on the 9 systems of the Amber suite, and on STMV ×1, ×4, ×8, ×11 (436 at the 9.5 Å reach of the replicas, 662 at the suite's 11 Å); `mdir emit` of main and the branch byte-identical (module and lowered) on the 9 suite systems and STMV ×1; JAC in the deterministic mode (2,000 steps) gives the same log rows. *Measured* (RTX 4090 node, 16 cores, main and the branch in one job, `mdir run` to the first row of energies): STMV ×1 from 61.4 to 18.3 s, ×4 from 216.1 to 41.2 s, ×8 from 446.3 to 73.0 s, ×11 from 569.2 to 97.3 s; the count itself on the login node (AMD EPYC 7543), the serial search against the new one with 32 threads: 45.3 against 0.16 s at ×1, 551.2 against 1.91 s at ×11, and 7.04 against 0.58 s on one thread at Factor IX. The setup at ×11 is now mostly reading the `.gro` (about 59 s) and the `.top` (14 s), `validateTopology` (7 s), and compiling (10 s).
