# D196

Recorded 2026-10-06.

**A Python simulation persists across runs of any number of steps: its programs take the counts of their loops as arguments, the first begins as `mdir run` does and the second continues the state of the last segment as a run continues its checkpoint, and the periods of coupling are counted from the first step.** M2 item 3 (#85). `mdir.Simulation(program)` compiles from the program's prepared model, refusing a stale one; `run(n)` returns the steps taken. A period of coupling of C steps is C − k plain steps and k closing steps (k = 2 with the barostat of Trotter type, D92), and a run is cut into calls of the entry for the rest of a started period, whole periods, and the plain steps of its last period, so a segmented run couples at the same steps with the same random numbers, which are keyed by the absolute step; an end between the two Trotter steps is refused before any step. A call is a part of at most about 0.5 s, between which `request_stop()` and Ctrl-C are polled, with the GIL released while a part runs. Each part builds the buffers of its arguments anew: on a device the entry writes into the buffers of the host that it was given, which `mdir run` gives it once. `state()` returns read-only NumPy copies in input order (positions, velocities with their time offset, the forces the next step begins with, the cell). What a report can ask for is compiled and when it happens is chosen at run time (maintainer ruling on PR #86): the entry also takes whether the last step of the call is a step of energy, the one `mdir run` takes at a row of its log — closing a period of coupling with its coupling, or plain inside a period — and `run(n, energy=True)` leaves that row in `state().energies`, at no evaluation of its own. The continuation program is built from the system at its first step, so that its constants and neighbor structures do not depend on the state a run has reached. A failure during a part (positions that are not numbers at a neighbor build on a device, reported through a handler of the runtime while the part runs to its end; a state that is not numbers at the end of a part; a cell below twice the cutoff) raises `SimulationError` and leaves the state from before the part; Python stays alive. One simulation runs at a time in a process; the first GPU simulation fixes the device. Each ELF host module keeps its entry, constructors, and destructors in one text section so its exception-frame range cannot overlap another module and leave a freed registration in the unwinder after engine destruction. Refused for now: minimization, NPT with a coupling period of 1, NPT in a triclinic cell. *Evidence.* On the dipeptide in water with PME, 1 + 7 + 13 steps against 21: in double precision on the CPU and on a GPU, NVE, NVT, NPT, and leapfrog NVT agree within 9e-16 nm and 6e-13 nm/ps, where the thermostat moves the velocities by 5e-2 nm/ps; in mixed precision within the error of mixed precision itself. Repeated runs are identical bit for bit in the deterministic mode. The energies of a run of 20 steps that ends with a step of energy, and of one of 10, equal the rows that `mdir run` writes at those steps to every printed digit, for NVE, NVT, and NPT in double and mixed precision, on the CPU and on a GPU; after a boundary they agree within 2e-9 of each value in double and 2e-6 in mixed. Not bit for bit across a boundary: each part puts the particles in order and builds the structures anew, as `mdir run` does at a checkpoint. See [python-segments.md](../python-segments.md).
