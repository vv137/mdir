# D163b

Recorded 2026-10-03.

**Brownian dynamics is an integrator: `integrator = "BROWNIAN"` with `friction` $\gamma$ of `[dynamics]` in 1/ps, at the temperature of `[ensemble]`, which must be `NVT`, and with no `[thermostat]`.** The step of [[Ermak1978]](../references.md#ermak1978) without hydrodynamic interactions, $\Delta\mathbf x = \Delta t\,\mathbf F/(m\gamma) + \sqrt{2k_BT\Delta t/(m\gamma)}\,\mathbf R$, Euler and Maruyama for the overdamped equation, with $\mathbf R$ from Philox in the kernel, stream 2 of the particle and the step, as Langevin dynamics draws them (D135; the generator is now `emitNoise`, which both take). It is written as a velocity that the drift takes over $\Delta t$, so the constraints, the correction of the velocity, and the virtual sites are those of the other steps, with no kick; the velocities stored are $\Delta\mathbf x/\Delta t$, whose kinetic energy, about $2/(\gamma\Delta t)$ times that of the temperature, the log shows as it is; there is no conserved energy. The user chose `friction` under `[dynamics]` rather than from a `[thermostat]` of Langevin dynamics: any `[thermostat]`, a `[barostat]`, an ensemble other than `NVT`, `center_of_mass_interval`, and `friction` of `[dynamics]` with another integrator are errors. A program of a step that did not read its velocities made `md-exec-assign-storage` assert that the buffers of a loop are conserved; the drift velocity of a particle of mass 0 is its old velocity, which keeps them an operand. Validated on 125 atoms in harmonic wells of their own ($k$ = 2 kcal/mol/Å², 150 K, $\Delta t$ = 10 fs) against the stationary variance of the discrete step, $(k_BT/k)/(1 - a/2)$ with $a = k\Delta t/(m\gamma)$: the mean energy of the wells 62.379 ± 0.157 kcal/mol at $\gamma$ = 1/ps ($a = 0.21$) against 62.429 (canonical 55.890), on the CPU in double and in mixed precision, and 56.381 ± 0.146 at 10/ps against 56.482; the correlation of energies 10 steps apart 0.657 ± 0.012 against $(1-a)^{20} = 0.655$; on a GPU in mixed precision (`brownian.test`, `brownian-gpu.test`). On the dipeptide in water with SETTLE and SHAKE it runs at 0.1 fs with $\gamma$ = 50/ps; at 2 fs the drift of a hydrogen, $\Delta t\,F/(m\gamma) \approx$ 0.2 nm, breaks the constraints, which is the mobility of light atoms, not a defect. *Amended after review:* the log and the file of energies of a Brownian run drop the total, the kinetic energy, the temperature, and the conserved energy, which the displacements over a step would give 2/(γΔt) times too large, and the pressure takes the kinetic energy of the bath, $\tfrac12N_fk_BT$, that of the momenta that the overdamped limit leaves Maxwellian; with constraints, a step whose random displacement of the lightest atom, $\sqrt{2k_BT\Delta t/(m_\text{min}\gamma)}$, exceeds 0.005 nm, a twentieth of a bond of hydrogen, is warned of (`brownian_step`), with the longest step that does not: on the dipeptide 0.0141 nm at 2 fs, 0.0031 at 0.1 fs. *Amended by the fix of #19:* `md-exec-assign-storage` now frees the buffer of a carried field that a loop does not read and of a result that nothing uses, so the step no longer reads the velocities, and a particle of mass 0 does not drift.
