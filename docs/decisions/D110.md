**On a device, a loop over particles, the loops over the tuples of a disjoint union that read it, and the loop over particles that reads them are one kernel, with a second for the tuples whose members lie in two warps.** The kick and drift before the constraints, SETTLE and the two sets of SHAKE (one kernel since D83), and the loop after them took three kernels and wrote and read their values in between: 83 + 98 + 64 µs a step for the positions and 183 µs for the velocities on Cellulose. A thread now computes the loop before for its particle and keeps its values in registers. The member at place 0 of a tuple whose members all lie in its warp gathers what the kernel of the tuple takes of each member by shuffles from their lanes, computes the tuple, and each member takes its values back from that lane and computes the loop after. A particle is in one tuple of the union at most, so one set of shuffles serves all the sets; a lane loads its own value of each input once, narrowed where the kernel of the tuple narrows it at once (the kernels compute in f32 in mixed precision), and takes its position relative to that of the member at place 0 in f64 before narrowing it, so that the member at place 0 converts nothing. A tuple whose members lie in two warps (about 2 waters in 32, whose three members straddle a boundary of 32 places) is listed by its member at place 0, and its members write their values of the loop before; a second kernel, a thread for each listed tuple, finishes it. Handled by every thread of the second kernel, such tuples held each warp for the latency of a tuple (175 µs for the positions); listed, but with the loop before computed again for each member, they cost its f64 divisions (47 µs, now 35). The destinations of the tuples that nothing reads after the loop after are not written. A loop that is already the loop after of one run is not the loop before of the next (a minimization). The driver orders the particles by the position of an anchor, the oxygen of a water or the center of a group of SHAKE, so that the members of a group stay next to each other: by their own positions a water could straddle cells and then warps (275 tuples across warps among the first 2000 particles of Cellulose, against 42). Cellulose NVE (RTX 3090): 124 + 35 µs a step for the positions and 102 + 24 for the velocities, from 245 and 183, with a launch of 1.7 µs each that clears the count of the list; 2859 µs a step of the device, from 2981. JAC: −58167.2548 kcal/mol after 10 steps, against −58167.2578; the total energy changes by 3e-5 of its value over 500 steps of JAC and by 1e-4 over 500 of Cellulose. All tests pass on both targets; the CPU shares the order by anchors and the narrowed loads of the kernels of the tuples, not the fused kernel. *Amended 2026-10-02: a virtual site takes the anchor of the first atom that places it. Ordered by its own position, the extra point of an OPC water near a face of a cell of the order fell into another cell than its atoms: ubiquitin in 5700 OPC waters put 232 tuples of virtual sites a step in the second kernel, 13.8 µs of one block (Nsight Compute), now 1.5 µs; 525 to 543 ns/day.* *Amended 2026-10-06 by D204:* in the deterministic mode the joined kernel takes the positions of the members as stored, so that the displacements between members are those of the kernels of the loops alone.
