# D111

Recorded 2026-10-01.

**A warp of the first kernel of an integration run takes whole groups, and the second kernel is one block.** Under D110 a group that straddled a boundary of 32 places (about 2 waters in 32) went to the second kernel, whose time is the latency of a group whatever their number: on JAC it took 28 and 20 µs a step for the two runs, and the runs 40 and 31 µs against 23 and 19 for the three loops apart; the suite gave 626 ns/day on JAC against 676 before D110 (FactorIX, Cellulose, and STMV faster), and 592 against 642 with `fuse-integration=false` (2000 steps). Warp w now takes the particles from b(S w) to b(S (w + 1)), with S the stride, 32 less the widest arity less one (30 on Cellulose, 29 on JAC with its methyls), and b(n) = n moved up past the group that n is inside if the members of that group follow one another; a warp then holds 32 particles at most and no such group straddles two. The members of a group follow one another where their numbers do, the order being by the cell of the anchor and then by number (every group of the Amber suite and of the inputs of the tests); the lanes left over cost 1 − S/32 of the warps. The second kernel keeps the other groups. It is now one block, which reads the count of the list, takes the listed groups, and clears the count after a barrier: the count is allocated and cleared once where the function begins, and no kernel clears it before each run. JAC (RTX 3090): 11.2 + 1.7 and 10.9 + 1.5 µs a step for the two runs; 654.7 ns/day over steps 1000 to 2000, against 591.9 under D110 and 642.1 with the loops apart. Cellulose: 132.6 + 2.1 and 105.3 + 2.1 µs, from 161 and 128 under D110; 2826 µs a step of the device, from 2859. `integration-gpu.test` runs propane with its hydrogens after its carbons, whose groups do not follow one another and some of which the second kernel takes, against the loops apart and the CPU, in double precision. The option `fuse-integration` of `convert-md-exec-to-gpu` keeps the loops apart.
