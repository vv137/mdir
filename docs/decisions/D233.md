# D233

Recorded 2026-10-08.

**The Python model takes terms of the absolute positions, `mdir.ExternalTerm` in `System.external_terms`: the control file's `[[energy.external]]` (D148) with `x`, `y`, `z` in nm and the energy in kJ/mol, over a mask or an array of particle indices, with constants.** Issue #188 (maintainer's decision on PR #213, Q1): the walls of D189, whose forces give the osmotic pressure, could not be written in Python. The model converts the expression at the boundary as it does those of pair and tuple terms and hands the term to the code of the control file, which resolves the mask and emits the sum over particles; `scaling` is `ExternalScaling.None_` or `ExternalScaling.Cell` (D154) and must be given with a barostat, as in the control file; the term takes `observe` (D232). Refused: both or neither of `selection` and `particles`, a particle twice or beyond the system, a constant named as a variable, an undeclared name. Not in this subset: a parameter with a value for each particle, and the time `t`. The fingerprint (D223) gains `[python] external_terms`, a hash of the terms, so `mdir run --continue` refuses a checkpoint of such a model and begins a stage from it. *Evidence* (`python-observe.test`, `-gpu.test`; CPU and RTX 3090, double and mixed): two walls of 4184 kJ/mol/nm² on the 1146 atoms of the waters of the dipeptide have the energies 5035.071714 and 3389.462793 kcal/mol of a NumPy sum to $8.9\times10^{-16}$ (the first is the value written out by hand in `observables.test`) and its forces to $1.2\times10^{-15}$ of the largest; after 10 steps in the deterministic mode the energy file equals that of `mdir run` with the same `[[energy.external]]` terms byte for byte, and the positions, velocities, and forces those of its checkpoint to the bit; particles given by index equal the mask. `mdir run --continue` refuses the checkpoint of the Python model, naming `[python] external_terms`. See [python-observe.md](../python-observe.md#terms-of-the-positions-d233).
