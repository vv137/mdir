# D258

Recorded 2026-10-15.

**The entry of a program declares the buffers of the model that it takes read-only (`mdrt.read_only` on the masses, the fields of the particles, the tables, and the members and fields of the tuple sets); `md-exec-assign-storage` never takes such a buffer for another field or for a copy back from a device; and `check-read-only-arguments`, where the lowering to loops or to a device ends, proves that the code writes none of them or fails the compile. A start of an activation then hands the entry the arrays of the program without a copy, keeps their conversions into the type of the entry where it is given the same array, and keeps the buffers of the state, written anew at every start.** Issue #326, amends D215, under which every start copied the state and the whole model into new buffers of the host, because the entry could write what it was given. *Measured first* (`main` f6f4d39 with marks of the arguments by kind; `scripts/benchmarks/activation/start.py --only update --marks`; PME, flexible, the length of a soft pair term tunable; one RTX 4090, mixed precision; ms a start): the arguments took 0.46, 1.80, and 11.7 ms on JAC, Factor IX, and Cellulose of the Amber suite, of starts of 2.2, 6.2, and 31 ms. On Cellulose the positions and velocities 4.1 (f64, copied one number at a time), the forces 2.5 (converted to f32), the masses 0.69, the fields of the particles 0.90, the members of the tuple sets 1.5, their fields 1.8, the identities 0.22, the tables 0.01; in double precision, with no conversion, the same (12.0 ms): the cost was the copy into new memory. *Why it copied.* On the CPU `md-exec-assign-storage` owned every buffer of a field that the function was given, so a loop could write another field into it once the field was dead, or write in place; on a device it kept them for the copies back (`mdrt.to_buffer`, a host call that is not `in_place`), keyed by type and set, so a field of a tuple set or the types of the particles could be written. Nothing was read-only by construction. *The guarantee* is the pass's decision, not a reading of it: the builder declares the arguments; the pass neither owns nor keeps them, and a field that a loop would write in place, or a copy back, gets a buffer of its own; the new pass follows every use of each declared argument through views and into the functions of the module and accepts only reads (memory effects that are reads, the shape, and a call of a declared function whose parameter is declared read-only too: the functions of the runtime that take the members, which read them through a `const int32_t *`). A write, a release, a buffer carried by a loop or returned, an address taken, a call of a function without the declaration, and an op whose effects are unknown fail the compile with the argument and the op named: a proven read-only and a failure are never confused. A program taken from the compile cache carries the proof (its key holds the text with the attributes and the pipeline with the pass). The state and the identities are not declared (the program on the CPU works in them). *The remedy*, cheapest first as the issue listed: (1) a copy of f64 is one `memcpy` into memory that is kept; (2) the arrays that the entry takes in their own type (the tables; the members and fields of the tuple sets and the masses, `SharedArray` since D256; the fields of the particles in double precision) are handed as they are, with a share held by the activation; (3) a conversion (the fields in f32, the types in i32) is kept with a share of the array it was made from and taken only where the start is given that array (`SharedArray::shares`: a comparison, and the array cannot change while shared), and the buffers of the state and the identities are kept and every number of them written at every start. Under `MDRT_CHECK_KEPT_TUPLES` (no new variable) each conversion taken as kept is made anew and compared, and each buffer handed without a copy is copied at the start and compared at the end of its activation; a difference ends the process naming the buffer. *Result* (`main` f6f4d39 and this head alternated six times in one job on one RTX 4090, rounds 2 to 6, mean and standard deviation): the arguments of a start 0.439 to 0.060, 1.762 to 0.182, and 11.51 to 1.88 ms; `gradient()` again 2.018 (0.007) to 1.633 (0.018), 5.971 (0.054) to 4.245 (0.046), and 30.21 (0.04) to 20.77 (0.50) ms; an update with its evaluation 4.384 to 3.616, 12.37 to 8.92, and 62.03 to 42.81 ms; a start in a new order after 20 steps 1.909 to 1.509, 5.186 to 3.460, and 25.34 to 15.65 ms; a step of a run of 20,000 0.1178 and 0.1179, 0.3873 and 0.3873, 1.9085 and 1.9077 ms. *Memory.* One set of buffers of the state, the identities, and the conversions for each simulation, which every start allocated and freed before; the handed arrays take none. *What the declaration changes in `mdir run`* (the design review): on JAC, Factor IX, Cellulose, and STMV, NVE and NPT, the module text is that of `main` but for the attribute, and the code differs in one buffer: on the CPU the identities in a new order were permuted into the buffer of the types of the particles, an argument, and now into a buffer of their own (4 bytes a particle, allocated once); on a GPU the copy of the identities at the end of the run went to the buffer of the types and now to that of the identities. Op for op the rest is the same once values are renamed, so a step is what it was. *Validated.* `check-read-only.mlir` and `check-read-only-invalid.mlir` (the reads accepted, eleven uses refused with their messages); `assign-storage-read-only.mlir` (a buffer of its own on the host and on a device where, without the attribute, the pass takes the argument); every program that the suite compiles, on the CPU and a GPU, passes the check. The sequence of `python-start-arguments*.test` (steps, `gradient()` again, an evaluation, updates of each kind of tunable and back, updates between steps, commits of borrows of the charges and on the CPU of the state, a second simulation of the program between the starts of the first, a checkpoint and a continued run, a frame evaluator beside its sampler) under the check, against a simulation whose starts copy everything (`Simulation._keep_start_arguments`, private), and against `main`'s own build: 494 records on the CPU and 464 on a GPU in double and mixed precision, and the 406 of the update sequence of D254, none different; a byte of a handed buffer written over by the test ends the process under the check, naming it. Nothing user-visible; the module text gains the attribute, so the entries of the compile cache are compiled anew once. [jit-invariants.md](../jit-invariants.md#what-a-start-gives-the-entry-d258).
