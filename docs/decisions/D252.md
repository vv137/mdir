# D252

Recorded 2026-10-13.

**A frame evaluator gives, where they are asked, the gradients of the energy of a frame in the positions and in the strain, $\partial U/\partial\mathbf x_i = -\mathbf F_i$ and $\partial U/\partial\varepsilon_{ab} = -\mathsf W_{ba}$ under $\mathbf x \to (\mathsf 1 + \boldsymbol\varepsilon)\mathbf x$ of the positions and the cell vectors; one argument, `gradients=`, a tuple of names among `"tunables"`, `"positions"`, and `"strain"`, replaces `gradient=`; `mdir.torch.evaluate` differentiates the energy in the positions and the cell, and every second derivative is refused.** Issue #313, slice 3 of the frame evaluator (#138), [python-frames.md](../python-frames.md#the-gradients-in-the-positions-and-the-strain-d252); the seven questions of PR #314 were decided as proposed but the first, where the maintainer took one argument in place of a second one beside `gradient`. `FrameEvaluator.evaluate(positions, cells, tilts, *, gradients=("tunables",))`: the default is what an evaluator returned before, the derivative in the tunables (`jacobian`, `vjp`) and nothing else; `gradients=()` asks for none; `gradient=` is gone, with no alias and no hint. `FrameEnergies.gradients` is a read-only mapping of `"positions"`, `(K, N, 3)` in kJ/mol/nm in the order of the input and in the type in which the program keeps its forces (float64 in `double`, float32 in `mixed`, float64 in `mixed` with the deterministic mode, #102; no conversion and no `dtype`), and `"strain"`, `(K, 3, 3)` float64 in kJ/mol, nine components for orthorhombic and triclinic programs alike, not symmetrized and not divided by the volume. Both are derivatives of the energy that `energy` returns (D210) with what the host adds at the volume of the frame, whose isotropic virials enter as a third on each axis: the virial that a run adds for a tail is $3(E_\text{tail} + E_\text{sh})$, the derivative of the tail and of the estimate of the shift together, so that `virial` $= -\mathrm{tr}\,\partial U/\partial\boldsymbol\varepsilon$ in a program without constraints. With constraints `virial` has the term of the constraint forces besides, which is of the pressure of the constrained dynamics and no derivative of $U$: the gradient in the strain leaves it out, a pressure takes `virial`, and the gradient in the positions is the plain gradient in every coordinate, since a frame is taken as given. The row of a virtual site is zero and its atoms hold the spread. For a program with restraints to reference positions the gradient in the strain is refused (`UnsupportedError`): their virial is taken about the references (D124), the derivative when the references deform with the cell, which those of a frame do not (the diagonal differs from central differences of the energy by up to 51 of 51,680 kJ/mol on the dipeptide with its heavy atoms restrained); the gradient in the positions is given. `terms="dependent"` refuses both and lists `"gradients"` in `unavailable` (T3 of D243). The request travels with each frame through `@mdrtFrameBoundary`, which takes a second buffer, and the program hands the forces and the nine components of the virial to the host through `@mdrtWriteFrameForces` and `@mdrtWriteFrameVirial`, from an evaluation of their own (`request [forces]`, or `[forces, virial]`) in a loop of one iteration or none: a frame that asks for nothing evaluates and copies nothing more, its other outputs are the same to the bit whatever is asked, and calls that ask and calls that do not share an activation. Taking them from the evaluation of the frame, written first, cost every frame 3% to 5% on the CPU, asked or not (a consumer of the whole forces or of the nine components changes its kernels), which the ruling on the cost does not allow; a frame that asks pays one more evaluation of the forces instead (on the CPU in double precision, the dipeptide: 45.0 ms without, as on `main`, 58.0 ms with `"positions"`, 60.3 ms with `"strain"` or both; on one RTX 4090 in mixed precision, at 64 frames a call, the dipeptide 0.298 ms without against 0.300 ms on `main`, 0.340 ms with `"positions"`, 0.372 ms with `"strain"`, 0.388 ms with both, and JAC 0.704 ms without against 0.701 ms, 0.841 ms, 0.891 ms, 0.960 ms). The text of a program of frames changes by these lines, so the kept code of an evaluator is compiled anew once; no kernel, no function of a device runtime, and no other program changes. In `mdir.torch.evaluate`, positions, cells, and tilts that are tensors requiring a gradient are inputs of `mdir::frame_energies`; the function differentiated is $U(\mathbf x, H)$ at fixed Cartesian positions, with $\partial U/\partial H = H^{-\mathsf T}(\partial U/\partial\boldsymbol\varepsilon - \sum_i\partial U/\partial\mathbf x_i\otimes\mathbf x_i)^{\mathsf T}$ for the six numbers of the cell, the sum taken on the host in f64; the gradients as outputs carry a backward that raises unless detached (the pattern of Q5 of D240), so that a loss on the forces or the stress can be evaluated but cannot yet train the tunables (force matching, stage 4 of #138, deferred by the maintainer); and the products of the backward are operations (`mdir::frame_vjp`, `mdir::frame_input_vjp`) whose own backward raises `UnsupportedError`, in place of PyTorch's `RuntimeError` for `theta` and of the zero that a multiplication of tensors would give for the positions. Validated (`python-frame-gradients*.test`, on the CPU and on a GPU, one RTX 4090, whose numbers are of the size of the CPU's, at most twice larger, in the rows of [python-frames.md](../python-frames.md#validation-of-the-gradients); relative to the largest entry; here the CPU, double and mixed): against central differences of the evaluator's energy in double precision, the gradient in the positions of the dipeptide in water $3.1\times10^{-8}$ and $2.5\times10^{-5}$, the nine components of the gradient in the strain of water in a sheared rhombic dodecahedron $1.8\times10^{-8}$ and $7.4\times10^{-6}$ (those below the diagonal with the deformed cell rotated back), with the correction for the dispersion and a net charge, a term of the absolute positions, rigid water, and virtual sites as well; the Coulomb part against torch-pme [[Loche2025]](../references.md#loche2025) by autograd in the positions and a strain, $4.8\times10^{-6}$ and $7.7\times10^{-7}$ on the dipeptide at 128 points, $5.2\times10^{-5}$ and $8.8\times10^{-6}$ in the triclinic cell at 64, $4.9\times10^{-6}$ and $8.0\times10^{-7}$ on JAC at 256; the whole potential against OpenMM 8.6.1, its forces $3.7\times10^{-6}$ and central differences of its energy less the shifts of its pairs under the nine strains $3.5\times10^{-6}$ at 128 points; the gradient in the cell through autograd against central differences, $2.0\times10^{-6}$ for the edges and $3.2\times10^{-8}$ for the tilts, and `gradcheck`.
