# D230

Recorded 2026-10-08.

**A program compiled with `System.tunable_gradient` evaluates, when `Simulation.tunables.gradient()` asks, the derivative of the potential that the forces sample in every entry of every tunable, $\partial U/\partial\boldsymbol\theta$ with the named structure of $\boldsymbol\theta$: `md-differentiate` gives the derivative of a potential in an argument that is a field of the particles, every site derivative leaves the program as such a field through a seed, a field of zeros added with weights where a kernel reads a parameter, and the host applies the chain rule beside `applyTunables` and adds the derivatives of the constants of the energy.** Issue #203, the first implementation step of M2b (#138, D195). The compile input is off by default and a program without it is that of D213 text for text; the method returns a read-only mapping of the names to $(M,)$ float64 arrays in kJ/mol per unit of the tunable, with the energy it is the derivative of, the version, the step, and the names that are zero by proof (maintainer's decisions on PR #205: a method, an opt-in compile input, the chain rule on the host and not in the IR of a second potential as D213 had written, the shifted potential of D210 and not the cut energy that the run reports, #188 left separate). The potential `@tunable` is `@energy` shifted at the cutoff, with the tunable constants of pair terms as numbers (the rule of D161 and D189) and the seeds as fields; the entry evaluates it in a loop at its start that runs once when the host asks, so the steps are unchanged. `derivative(n)` of a field: a sum over a relation gives a gather over it with the derivative of the kernel in the value of the central particle and no exchange contract, a sum over tuples a gather over them, a sum over particles a map; a field that no sum takes has zeros, and any other use (a reciprocal sum, a map) is an error naming the op. Seeds: a per-type $\sigma$ or $\epsilon$ takes the Jacobian of the combining rule as weights by pairs of types, 0 for pairs set apart or taken by a pair tunable, and the host adds the field over the particles of each type; the table by pairs takes one pair in each row of a seed, as many seeds as pairs share a type [[CurtisPowellReid1974]](../references.md#curtispowellreid1974); a parameter of a tuple term takes the seed at one member, tuples that share it taking different seeds; the charges are their own field, the 1-4 pairs forming $fs_Cq_iq_j$ from them in `@tunable`. No new reduction or lowering: the loops accumulate per particle as they do the forces, in f64 because the host function declares f64 buffers. The host adds the tail and the shift estimate of a pair term in its constants by the Richardson differences of its quadrature (D209) and the correction for the dispersion with its shift estimate in $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$ by closed form through the same weights. A dependence without a rule is an error of `mdir.compile` naming the tunable; a value that is not finite is an error of `gradient()`; a per-type value of 0 under $\sqrt{xy}$ is refused with the remedy. *Not in this decision:* the charges with particle mesh Ewald, refused at compile; they follow as a decision of their own on #203, a fourth result of `md.reciprocal`, the potential of the grid at the particles, only in a program compiled with the derivative (maintainer's decisions on PR #205). A live view or writable borrow (D220, D229) refuses `gradient()` as it refuses an evaluation; a name in `g.zero` is a zero by proof from the expressions, and an entry may be 0 at a state without it. The Python model has no reaction field. *Evidence.* On the CPU and a GPU, in double and mixed precision (`python-tunable-gradient*.test`): springs over tuples that share particles against NumPy to $4\times10^{-16}$ (double) and $3\times10^{-7}$ (mixed); a constant of $-c/r^8$ under a plain cutoff with its tail and shift estimate against NumPy and closed forms to $1.7\times10^{-16}$ and $1.9\times10^{-5}$ (the kernel's table of $r^{-8}$ in f32); per-type (geometric) and pair $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$ with a pair set apart, the shifted Lennard-Jones with the correction and its shift estimate against a NumPy sum to $2.5\times10^{-11}$ and $1.3\times10^{-6}$; the charges under a Coulomb cutoff, tied in 25 entries, with the 1-4 pairs against NumPy to $7.7\times10^{-16}$ and $1.2\times10^{-7}$; every entry against central differences of the energy to $1.8\times10^{-9}$ and $1.2\times10^{-4}$; after 200 steps of leapfrog under a barostat, $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$ by pairs against central differences to $3.6\times10^{-11}$ and $7.7\times10^{-6}$; twelve steps through the derivative equal those without it to the bit, and a simulation continued from a checkpoint gives the derivative of the one that wrote it to the bit. Without tunables JAC runs at main's rate (NVE 852 against 850 ns/day, NPT 784 against 785) and the modules and lowered IR of five programs without the compile input equal main's; with it a Python simulation of JAC steps in 0.257 ms as without, and `gradient()` takes 10 ms against 9 ms for `run(0, energy=True)`. *Amended (#256):* the derivatives of one evaluation are independent first derivatives of one energy, and the derivative in a field follows the ops of the potential alone: every other op of the function was added by a derivative requested before it (the copy of a sum whose kernel yields its derivative in a number, which takes the fields that the sum gathers; the gathers of the forces) and is not a use by the energy. Before, the charges with a tunable constant of a pair term compiled and failed at the first simulation, the derivative in the charges refusing the sum of the derivative in the constant as a use "within another op". The promise of this row, an error of `mdir.compile`, is kept by `compile` itself: it does not lower (D224), so it runs `md-check-exchange` and `md-differentiate` on the program and on the program of the segments of a simulation, lowers nothing, and raises `InputError` with the diagnostic of the pass and the name of the tunable; this holds for the forces, the virial, and the derivatives of `observe` as well. *Evidence.* 28 programs with the charges (free, or tied in 25 entries; PME or a Coulomb cutoff) and constants of a pair term, the table by pairs, per-type $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$, parameters of tuples, an external term that reads the charges, an observed constant, or all of them, on the CPU and a GPU: every derivative against central differences within $6.2\times10^{-9}$ in double precision and $1.2\times10^{-4}$ in mixed, and equal to the bit to that of the program that declares the charges alone or the others alone (`python-tunable-gradient-mixed*.test`).
