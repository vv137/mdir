# D283

Recorded 2026-10-18.

**On a device, a kernel of loops over tuples evaluated once adds what the tuples of a block give each particle in a table in block memory, and each particle of the table to the destinations once a block.** Issue #408 ([device-tuples.md](../device-tuples.md), Section 11). *Measured first* (Cellulose NVE, one RTX 4090): the kernel of the bonded terms, the 1-4 pairs, and the excluded pairs took 222.6 µs a step; each set as a kernel of its own cost 17 to 24 ps an atomic addition, whatever parameters it read, the excluded pairs 89.9 µs; with plain stores in place of the atomics (for timing) 65 µs, on STMV 154 of 444: about 70% was the global atomics, and the excluded pairs by rows took 93 µs against 90. A block of 128 consecutive tuples touches 0.237 distinct particles a member on Cellulose (0.309 JAC, 0.269 STMV). *The kernel*: a table of 128 × the largest arity slots, rounded up to a power of two (8 KB for forces in f32 with dihedrals), keys found by a multiplicative hash and the next slots in turn [[Knuth1998]](../references.md#knuth1998), claimed by a compare-and-swap; values added in block memory; after a barrier one global atomic a component for each particle of the table. A search gives up after 8 slots and adds that value to the destination directly (a block of JAC with 494 particles in 512 slots took the kernel from 12 to 42 µs without the bound). A run of fewer than 100,000 tuples keeps the atomics, the host choosing between the two kernels by the count (`tuple-block-least`): with the table JAC (55,000 tuples, every block resident at once) ran 1.5% slower on an RTX 4090 and 3% on an RTX PRO 6000, Factor IX (165,000) 2.3% and 1% faster. A run whose table would exceed 48 KB, the loops with global sums, and every loop of a run that holds the forces of a term over centers (D194) keep their atomics; the compile takes up to 0.2 s longer. 8 KB a block; 47 registers against 44, both allocated as 48, so the occupancy is unchanged. The option `tuple-block-sums` of `convert-md-exec-to-gpu`, on by default; `MDIR_TUPLE_SUMS=atomics` turns it off and `=blocks` takes the table at any size (the tests, `tuple-block-sums-gpu.test`, also under the sanitizer). *Validated* (the settings of the suite, one step, one RTX 4090): the forces with and without the switch differ by 2.6 × 10⁻⁷ to 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ of the RMS force in mixed precision (largest 2.4 × 10⁻⁴), as two runs with atomics differ from each other (9.7 × 10⁻⁷ to 1.1 × 10⁻⁶, largest 3.0 × 10⁻⁴), and by 4 × 10⁻¹⁶ to 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ in double; the potential energy to 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ and the virial to 10⁻⁸; the deterministic mode, which evaluates no tuple once, equals `main` to the bit on the four systems. *Measured* (alternated in one job; the tuple kernel in µs, atomics against block sums): RTX 4090 (ada-003) Factor IX 24.8 → 16.4, Cellulose 220.8 → 84.4, STMV 439.1 → 189.4; RTX PRO 6000 (ada-004) 17.0 → 12.3, 151.5 → 81.0, 254.1 → 160.3. The Amber suite against `main` (three rounds, median ns/day ratio), RTX 4090 and RTX PRO 6000: JAC (four inputs) 0.999 to 1.000 and 1.000 to 1.002, Factor IX 1.025 to 1.027 and 1.011 to 1.013, Cellulose 1.089 to 1.103 and 1.034 to 1.037, STMV 1.028 to 1.051 and 1.020. *Not taken*: the parameters of the tuples stored in f32 (the same bits, since every load is narrowed at once; about 4 µs a step on Cellulose with the block sums), the excluded pairs in the loop over pairs, and the tuples in the order of their first member (0.10 particles a member; a follow-up).
