# D235

Recorded 2026-10-08.

**On a device the particles of a cell of the neighbor matrix take their places by rank: each particle counts the particles of its cell with a smaller index, one thread per particle, in place of a sort by insertion in one thread per cell.** The width of a cell is bounded below and chosen by the cost of the search, so a small system has few cells of many particles: 1,410 particles (ethanol in 467 waters, a cell of 25 Å, a reach of 10 Å) have 8 cells of about 176, and the insertion sort of a cell, whose slots the atomic additions fill in no order, moved thousands of entries in one thread. In a trace of that system at constant pressure (RTX 3090 at 300 W, mixed precision, #231) that kernel took 852 µs at each build, every 5.2 steps with a skin of 1 Å: 164 of the 318 µs of a step, of which the device was busy 268. Now a kernel with a thread per place reads its particle, counts the entries of the cell that are smaller, and stores the particle at that rank in a buffer of the build; a second copies the buffer back, with the positions. The order is the same, by index within a cell, so the matrix, the forces, and a run in the deterministic mode are those of before to the bit: 100,000 steps of that system at constant pressure end at the same energies, pressure, and volume. The work of a cell stays quadratic in its particles, as the insertion was, but is spread over them. **Measured** (GPU 0, mixed precision, constant pressure with the Langevin thermostat, 100,000 steps, one run each): 0.291 to 0.115 ms a step for the 1,410 particles (594 to 1,506 ns/day), 0.190 to 0.183 for 4,905 (a tripeptide in water), and 0.332 to 0.329 for the 23,558 of JAC with the matrix, whose cells hold few particles; the kernels of the sort take 177, 13, and 11 µs a step before and 2, 4, and 7 after. The groups of the Amber suite do not use this sort. The order of a spatial reordering (`@mdrt_gpu_spatial_order`), sorted by insertion as well, is left: it runs seldom.
