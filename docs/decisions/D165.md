# D165

Recorded 2026-10-03.

**Parameters of each particle are entries of the control file resolved by the driver into a value for every particle; a pair term gathers them as a field of the particles, and a term over tuples or of the positions takes them as parameters of its tuples or particles.** The custom forces of OpenMM give a parameter to each particle of a nonbonded force [[Eastman2017]](../references.md#eastman2017); MDIR's pair terms had only those of the topology (charges, Lennard-Jones) and its terms over tuples only parameters of each tuple. `[[energy.parameter]]` names a parameter and gives values by a mask of Amber, by the numbers of particles, for all, or as a list over the topology; entries of one name apply in the order of the file, so that a default and its exceptions read as they are meant, and a particle left without a value is an error rather than a silent 0. The terms take a parameter `w` by the place of a particle, `w1` and `w2` in a pair, `w1` to `wN` in a tuple, as the custom forces of OpenMM name them, and `w` itself in a term of the positions; a name may therefore not end in a digit, nor be one that the terms give. *Where the values go.* A pair term gathers `%p_pp_w` for both particles of a pair, a field of the particles that the reordering of the particles permutes with the others; a field is made only for a parameter that some pair term uses. A term over tuples takes `w2` as a parameter of each tuple, the value of its second particle, written by the driver when the tuples are known, and a term of the positions as one of each of its particles; neither needs a change of the IR. The reader tests the symmetry of a pair term in `w1` and `w2` with the other parameters (D137). Without a topology the parameters of the types already differ by particle, and `[[energy.parameter]]` is an error there. *Evidence.* On the dipeptide in water, a pair term of two such parameters and the charges, a bond and an angle weighted by them, and a term of the positions agree with CustomNonbondedForce, CustomCompoundBondForce, and CustomExternalForce of OpenMM 8.6.1 (Reference platform) to the printed digits of every energy and within 5.5e-13 of the largest force on the CPU in double precision, and within 2e-6 on the GPU in mixed precision (`test/Driver/particle-parameters.test`). *Functions of two and three arguments.* `[[energy.function]]` takes `values` as nested lists and `min`, `max` as lists; the table of the IR holds for each cell the $4^n$ coefficients of the product of cubics of Hermite that matches the values, the derivatives along each axis, and the mixed ones at its corners, the bicubic of [[Press2007]](../references.md#press2007) and the tricubic of [[Lekien2005]](../references.md#lekien2005); the derivatives at the points are those of natural (or periodic) splines along each axis, and the mixed ones of splines of those, in the order that OpenMM's Continuous2DFunction and Continuous3DFunction take, so that one table gives one function in both. The kernel computes the index of the cell from the floor of each place, the places in it, and Horner's rule over the lookups, and the derivative goes through the places only: the force is the gradient of the function that the table defines, and the patches are $C^1$ across their faces. A `discrete` function is the value at the nearest point, the arguments rounded and clamped as OpenMM's Discrete1D/2D/3DFunction do, which differs from returning 0 outside, first reported for this decision. *Evidence.* On the dipeptide, a pair term in a function of $r$ and of $s_1 + s_2$ and in a discrete table of the kinds of two particles, a term of the positions in a function of $x, y, z$, and a dihedral term in a function of two angles periodic in both agree with OpenMM 8.6.1 (Reference platform) to the printed digits of every energy and within 1.85e-12 of the largest force on the CPU, and within 6.3e-6 on the GPU in mixed precision (`test/Driver/tabulated-functions-nd.test`). *Compound terms.* `[[energy.compound]]` takes tuples of 2 to 9 particles and an expression in `distance()`, `angle()`, and `dihedral()` of any of their places, as OpenMM's CustomCompoundBondForce; the reader puts a name, `distance__1_3`, in place of each call and records the coordinate, and the term is a tuple set of that arity, ordered, whose `md.sum_tuples` lists the coordinates: the IR took any coordinate of any members already (`coordinates(dihedral(1, 2, 3, 4))` of CMAP), so the forces and the virial follow from its differentiation unchanged. A place is one digit, so that `w12` cannot mean two things; the positions themselves, OpenMM's `x1`, are left out. *Evidence.* On the dipeptide, a term over four particles in a distance, an angle, and a dihedral weighted by parameters of each particle, and one over five with members out of order, agree with OpenMM 8.6.1 to the printed digits and within 3e-16 of the largest force on the CPU (`test/Driver/compound-terms.test`). *Cost.* On JAC (GPU 0, mixed), a pair term of 0.4 of the Lennard-Jones of the topology runs at 0.211 ms a step, the force field's rate, as it shares the tables of the Lennard-Jones; weighted by `w1*w2` 0.224, and by `(1 + q1*q2)` 0.226: what costs is a term that is no longer a multiple of the Lennard-Jones, not the gathered field.
