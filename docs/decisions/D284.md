# D284

Recorded 2026-10-18.

**Where a refresh of a dual list of groups prunes, the first loop over pairs that takes its result, at the same positions and in the same cell, prunes for it in its own kernel, a warp for each group, from 4096 groups of the last build (20,480 on `sm_12x`): the warp prunes its group as the refresh did, waits for the stores of its lanes, and computes the pairs of the entries it kept as one stream of the group; the kernel of the active units follows. The refresh keeps the kernel of the frames.** Issue #362 (design first; the design and the prototype's numbers on #362 and PR #414). *Conditions* (`findPrunedInLoop`): the loop follows the refresh in the same block at the same positions and cell, no op between may write the positions (D268's shared frames), and no op between takes the refresh's result, so nothing reads the inner list before it is written; the refresh and the loop decide each pruning by one test of the groups on the host (`takesFusedPruning`). *Exactness*: an outer entry keeps bit $p$ iff its outer bit is set and $r^2 \le (R_\text{in} + \varepsilon)^2$ in f32 on the frames of the step, the code of the refresh's pruning (`emitPruneGroup`), so the inner list, its counts, and its active units are the pruning's to the bit; on the step itself the warp takes the pairs within $r_c < R_\text{in}$ of the entries kept, which are the pairs of the loop over the fresh inner list; the sums differ by the order of their additions only. `MDIR_CHECK_PRUNING` follows the loop where the loop prunes. The deterministic mode is unchanged: the groups refuse it (D261). *Not taken*, from the design: the loop over the outer list at every step, or over the outer units on a pruning step, each with the inner test as a by-product (the pair kernel on about twice the entries; Table 4.1 of the paper), and a warp a block of the outer list (blocks of the inner list half filled, or a scan across the blocks of a group, and rounds of 32 partly filled). *Measured* (mixed, `scripts/benchmarks/fused-prune/steps.py`, 4 rounds alternated, the first dropped, µs a step, the refresh's pruning → the loop's): RTX 4090, JAC 136.0 → 137.7, ubiquitin in OPC 188.7 → 193.7, TIP3P at 27,234 / 52,686 / 103,158 / 203,490 / 309,876 sites 155.0 → 157.0, 233.7 → 233.7, 405.0 → 388.3, 741.0 → 707.0, 1118.3 → 1050.3, Cellulose 1465.3 → 1375.7, OPC at 413,168 sites 1630.7 → 1528.3, STMV at 4 fs 4710.0 → 4458.0. The kernel takes the loop and 0 to 7% more (Cellulose 584.9 µs against 550.6 for the loop and 228.6 for the pruning's main kernel); the reading that fits, not measured with counters, is that the memory-bound pruning of some warps runs beside the arithmetic of others. It holds 94 to 96 registers, against 72 for each of the two. On an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q at its cap of 300 W it takes the sum of the two (591.2 against 415.0 and 167.5): JAC 116.3 → 124.0, TIP3P at 52,686 / 103,158 / 309,876 sites 173.0 → 178.7, 291.3 → 294.3, 896.3 → 898.3, Cellulose 1094.7 → 1093.0, STMV 3481.0 → 3441.3. *The threshold*, from the architecture of the compiled kernels: a warp a group leaves the device partly idle below about 3,300 groups on the RTX 4090 (equal at 3,292, 4% faster at 6,447), hence 4096; on the RTX PRO 6000 the step is equal from about 19,000 groups, and Factor IX (5,681) took 1 to 2% more from 4096, hence 20,480 on `sm_12x`. *Switch*: `MDIR_FUSED_PRUNE=off` (no such kernel compiled) or `always` (any size); the option `fuse-pruning` and `fuse-pruning-groups` of `convert-md-exec-to-gpu`. The log line of the prunings adds `, N of them in the loop over pairs`. *Tests*: `groups-gpu.test` (the loop that prunes against the matrix, every printed digit in double, NVE and NPT), `prune-check-gpu.test`, `fused-prune-gpu.test`, `test/Sanitizer/fused-prune-gpu.test`. No control-file key, format, or default of a key changes.
