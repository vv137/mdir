# D159

Recorded 2026-10-03.

**A function of the distance that no table holds stays in its kernel, and the square of the cutoff in f32 is pulled in so that the square root of every square that passes is four units in the last place below the cutoff in f32.** The two-body term of the mW water [[Molinero2009]](../references.md#molinero2009) in the Stillinger-Weber form, $A\epsilon\,(B(\sigma/r)^4 - 1)\exp(\sigma/(r - a\sigma))$ with the cutoff at $a\sigma$, failed both ways in mixed precision: with `fast_math`, the radial table of D94 could not hold the factor that vanishes at the cutoff with all its derivatives, and is not finite at it, and the compilation stopped as a defect; without it, the kernel in f32 compared $r^2$ with the square of the cutoff rounded to nearest, took $r = \sqrt{r^2}$, which rounded to $a\sigma$, and divided by zero (OpenMM's CUDA platform blows up the same way, as the survey of many-body terms found, ~/notes/mdir/many-body). `md-exec-simplify-distance` now tries the table of a function when it takes it out of a kernel and leaves it in the kernel, in the kernel's type, when the table misses the tolerance or is not finite, and `md-exec-expand-radial` evaluates such a function as itself rather than fail. The cutoff in f32 moves inward by some $5\times10^{-7}$ of itself, so that the product $a\sigma$ in f32, which may round a unit below the cutoff, is never reached. The four units are a heuristic, not a bound: a product of f32 constants lands within a unit of the cutoff when the constants are those of the cutoff, and four units cover that, but constants written to put the pole further inside (`a = 1.8000001`) still reach it. The same guard applies wherever a kernel evaluates a factor with a pole at its cutoff, the legs of a term over triplets among them. 512 and 4096 mW particles with the cutoff at $a\sigma$ = 4.3065 Å run 2 fs steps in mixed precision with and without `fast_math`, the energy changing by $3.4\times10^{-4}$ of its value over 3 ps, as in double precision, at 0.039 ms a step for 4096 against 0.11 in double; the energy at the start agrees with that of double precision to $3\times10^{-7}$. Found by the survey of many-body terms (task #56).
