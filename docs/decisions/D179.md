# D179

Recorded 2026-10-04.

**Moving a GPU reciprocal sum before a neighbor refresh requires independence from every intervening op, as well as dominance of its operands.** Amend D113 using the memory-effect and buffer-alias analysis of D87: an earlier allocation or loop-carried buffer does not prove that a later kernel has filled it. Crossing the mapped-charge producer in the step potential led to diverging energies and NaN positions on checkpoint restart (issue #26). Unknown effects and producers through buffer views also block the move; an independent particle kernel still permits it. Amend the workaround of D161: the step potential again maps scaled charges directly instead of constructing a `q_rec` field on the host. Compiler regressions cover the producer, an aliasing view, and an independent kernel; driver regressions compare checkpointed runs, same-state continuation, and a restart at another state against CPU double and central differences, on GPU mixed and double. With mapped charges restored before the fix, checkpointed mixed GPU rows differed from CPU double by up to 16.5 kcal/mol and the changed-state restart failed with nonfinite positions. Afterward, the worst mixed difference is $2\times10^{-5}$ kcal/mol; GPU double matches the printed CPU rows, and the mixed Coulomb derivative agrees with central differences within $6.12\times10^{-4}$ kcal/mol (tolerance $2\times10^{-3}$). All nine short Amber cases pass with standard and analytic bonded forces. A single 30,000-step JAC NVE run on GPU 0 measures 645.0 ns/day before and 645.5 after over the latter half (0.268 ms/step in both); this is not a repeated performance estimate.
