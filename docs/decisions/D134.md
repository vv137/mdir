# D134

Recorded 2026-10-02.

**The comparisons with pmemd.cuda run one model: its $\beta$, its grid, and its influence function.** pmemd takes $\beta$ from $\mathrm{erfc}(\beta r_c)/r_c$ = `dsum_tol` (0.39467 Å⁻¹ at $10^{-6}$, 0.34864 at $10^{-5}$, with $r_c$ = 8 Å), where MDIR's `tolerance` solves $\mathrm{erfc}(\beta r_c)$ = `tolerance` (0.4324 and 0.3904); pmemd.cuda takes grids of multiples of 4 clamped to powers of two near them (256 × 128 × 128 for Cellulose, where MDIR takes 270 × 126 × 126); and Amber scales the influence function by the aliasing factor ("OPTIMAL", pme.md, Section 1.1). Up to D133 the suite ran MDIR with its own three, a larger $\beta$ on the same grid, so with less accuracy in its reciprocal sum than pmemd's and more in its direct sum. `bench.py` now gives MDIR `beta`, `grid`, and `influence = "OPTIMAL"`, and GROMACS `ewald-rtol` of the same $\beta$ and `fourier-nx`, `-ny`, `-nz`. **Measured** (three repeats, RTX 3090 at 300 W, with D133): every system at 104.4% (Cellulose, NVE) to 131.8% (JAC at 4 fs) of pmemd.cuda; Factor IX at 110.0% and 109.2% with its 2283 dihedral terms, as before them (white paper, Table 10.2).
