# D106

Recorded 2026-10-01.

**A group whose excluded partners do not fit the memory of its warp takes its excluded pairs from their rows.** The build of the lists held at most 256 partners of the excluded pairs of a group (D89) and dropped the rest without a word: a group of 16 ring atoms of several chains of cellulose has more (258 seen, 288 possible from 18 partners an atom), rarely (none in 153 builds, one in the next few hundred), and its bonded pairs then took the full force of the pair potential, 1-3 pairs at 1.8 Å with a Lennard-Jones energy of hundreds of kcal/mol: a run of Cellulose NVE under groups stopped after 500 to 5500 steps, the constraints of a water or a sugar failing on the next step, positions NaN two steps later through PME, and the build then sorting every particle in one bin of its grid in one thread. The matrix was never affected. The count of a group's partners is known to every lane; where it exceeds the room, each candidate with pairs reads, for each particle of the group it pairs with, that particle's row of excluded pairs and clears the pair if the candidate is there: exact, slow, and rare. The room stays 256 (the sort of D105); a larger room would move the limit, not remove it. The test of the template adds a case of 18 excluded partners a particle. Three runs of 6000 steps of Cellulose complete (RTX 3090). The log of a run counts the groups that took the rows: 3 in 756 builds of about 25 000 groups each over 3000 steps of Cellulose, so the slow path costs nothing there; over the Amber suite only Cellulose has any (1 in 255 builds under NVE, 7 in 253 under NPT), JAC, FactorIX, and STMV none.
