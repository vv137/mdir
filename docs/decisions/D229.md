# D229

Recorded 2026-10-08.

**A Python simulation lends its state for writing through DLPack as a tracked borrow with an explicit commit: `Simulation.borrow()` returns the buffers of the positions and the velocities where the program keeps them, without the read-only flag, and buffers of the host with the edges of the cell and the values of the tunables; `Borrow.commit()` checks everything, advances the versions of the changed fields, and begins an activation from the committed state as an update of tunables does, so that the order, the neighbor structures, the derived fields, and the forces are built anew; a borrow excludes every other operation, and one that ends without a commit is undone from the copy of the last boundary.** Issue #136, M2a item 6, second part, which completes the item; amends D220, whose views stay read-only. The buffers have the types of the program (state f64 in double and mixed precision; forces, f32 in the mixed mode, are not lent), in the order of the program with `ids`; a commit converts nothing. A buffer of the state counts as written once `__dlpack__` was called on it in the borrow; the cell and each tunable if their bits differ at the commit. Exclusion: while the borrow or a tensor taken from it is alive, `run`, `minimize`, evaluations, updates of tunables, `view()`, `borrow()`, `state()`, and `save_checkpoint` raise `SimulationError`; `borrow()` is refused under a lease of a view and for a minimization. A commit is refused while a tensor is alive, waits for all work of the CUDA context, and checks before it changes anything: finite positions and velocities, edges finite and at least twice the cutoff, and for the tunables the checks of D213 with the rebuilt program equal to the compiled one text for text (`InputError`, nothing changed, the borrow still live). Then it does what an update of D213 does (D215): the state of the host is the committed one, the activation ends, and the next begins with `%first_call = 2`; `Simulation.versions` (`positions`, `velocities`, `cell`, `tunables`; P16) and `Simulation.commits` record it, and changed tunables advance their version and history as `update` does. An evaluation that fails undoes the commit. An upload in place that keeps the activation was not taken, since it would lose the equality with a simulation compiled from the committed state; a commit costs an evaluation and is not meant for every step. Abandon (`abandon()`, the end of a `with` block without a commit, a dropped borrow): once the last tensor is deleted, the buffers handed out are copied back from the snapshot of D215 and the activation continues. Maintainer rulings on PR #204: writes through a read-only view stay undefined and not detected, the borrow being the tracked path; the cell is the edges of an orthorhombic cell, a triclinic one refused as not supported yet (#206) (*amended by D238: `Borrow.tilt` lends the tilts*); the versions of the state are not recorded in checkpoints, and the fingerprint does not cover the state; leaving a `with` block without a commit abandons. Nothing on the path of a step, no kernel, and no program text changes. *Evidence* (`python-dlpack-write*.test`; dipeptide in water with PME, velocity Verlet at constant energy, deterministic; NumPy on the CPU, a ctypes writer with the CUDA driver API on an RTX 3090, PyTorch 2.14.1 on both; double and mixed): commits of one particle moved, of velocities scaled, of the cell with the positions scaled, of charges and a pair constant, and of positions with charges, each followed by 12 steps, equal to the bit a simulation compiled from the changed state (or `tunables.update`), where the move changes the forces by up to $5.4\times10^{3}$ kJ/mol/nm; an abandoned borrow, refused commits (a structural change of a tunable among them), and a commit whose evaluation fails equal a simulation without the borrow to the bit, no version advanced; consumer writes still under way on a PyTorch stream at the commit are taken (without the wait the velocities differ by 2.87 nm/ps); memory stays flat over 30 borrows (`python-memory*.test`). See [python-dlpack.md](../python-dlpack.md#writable-borrows-d229).
