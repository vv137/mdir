Recorded 2026-10-19.

**A domain's work runs through `md_exec`: `--convert-md-dist-to-md-exec`, the second executor of `md_dist`, gives the loops of each domain to `md_exec` over the domain's local rows and keeps the transfers of the reference executor, so that the CPU path and the device path per domain are the existing ones; `md_exec.build_neighbors`, `refresh_neighbors`, and `pair_for` take a center extent, `centers(%n)`, apart from the rows of the set, and `images(explicit)`, plain displacements for rows that carry their images; and `pair_for` takes the policy `(directed, atomic)`, the kernel's value at the center and $\sigma$ times it at the neighbor, both added atomically.** Issue #442, ML2 stage 1 (#31; [distributed-ml2.md](../distributed-ml2.md), Section 7.2). *Lowering*: each domain has two particle sets declared by the pass, its owned rows and its local rows (owned, then ghost), since `md-exec-assign-storage` takes one number of rows a set; a domain's rows are copied from the buffers of all domains and handed over by `mdrt.from_buffer`, and results come back by `mdrt.to_buffer`, the places where an MPI rank will send and receive; `md_dist.neighborhood` becomes a structure of the local rows with `centers` the owned rows and explicit images, `sum_pairs` a sum (weight 1/2 on a directed relation), a one-ended `gather_pairs` a loop `(directed, owner_only)` whose owned rows are the result, a two-ended one a loop `(directed, atomic)` whose prefix and tail are the owned and the ghost partial results (the kernel halved on a directed relation), the unique traversal the directed list with the kernel's value taken where the identity of the center (a field of `i64`) is the lesser, and `sum_particles` and `map_particles` loops over the owned rows; the domains are unrolled op by op, the code of a domain emitted from its number; the option `functions` leaves the other functions to the reference executor. *The search with explicit images* is the existing one, which wraps the rows and takes the minimum image: the minimum-image distance never exceeds the plain one, so it finds a superset, which the loop's cutoff on the plain displacement removes; rows 2.1 to 5.5 times as long as needed on the tests' grids. On the CPU a variant of the template, added only to a module that builds with `centers`, searches for the centers alone; on a device every row is searched and the loop skips the places of other rows. *Passes*: `md-exec-fuse-loops`, `md-exec-accumulate-destinations`, and `md-exec-assign-storage` carry the attributes over (a destination of a loop over centers or of `(directed, atomic)` starts from zeros, never overwritten); `md-exec-reuse-neighbors` leaves such a structure built at every evaluation; `md-exec-choose-neighbors` keeps it a matrix; a device lowers such a loop on its own, and in the deterministic mode refuses `(directed, atomic)`. The verifier refuses the attributes on groups, with exclusions, with another refresh policy than `always` or a dual list, with `(unique, atomic)`, and a loop whose centers or images differ from its structure's. *Validated* (`dist-lj-exec.mlir`, `dist-eam-exec.mlir`, `dist-many-body-exec.mlir` and their GPU twins: grids 1x1x1, 2x1x1, 3x2x1, 16x1x1, the unique traversal, the derivative's form, and owner-computes): against one domain, the reference executor on the same program, the Python references, central differences, and the adjoint identity, in `double` within $3.7\times10^{-15}$ (energy, force, virial; tolerance $10^{-10}$) on the CPU and an RTX 4090, and in `mixed` within $4.5\times10^{-6}$ (tolerance $10^{-5}$). The modules of existing programs, JAC NVE on a GPU in `mixed` and on the CPU in `double` included, are the same text.
