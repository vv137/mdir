# D150

Recorded 2026-10-03.

**The sums over few tuples cost less: a block adds all the sums of its kernel in one pass, a reduction of one part gives the results without a second kernel, a loop that evaluates each tuple once launches no more rows than it has tuples, the destinations of one loop that a loop over particles adds one after the other merge, loops over tuples fuse past the pure ops that use the first, and the host code is scheduled by the fast list scheduler of LLVM.** Measured on JAC (RTX 3090 at 300 W, 10,000 steps, mixed precision, GPU 0, three runs each, from the profile of D139): a bond between the centers of residues 1 to 5 and 150 to 159 took 0.28 ms a step against 0.21 without it (2.83 against 2.15 s); the nine sums of the term were reduced by two kernels of 10.2 and 9.0 µs, one `gpu.all_reduce` tree for each sum and each kernel; the loop of its forces gave nine fields, one for each sum, each overwritten, so that it ran over the rows of every particle with the bonds of the 1-4 pairs (+22 µs) and the integration read nine fields more (+8 µs). Now: (1) a warp adds each number by a butterfly of shuffles, its first lane writes the warp's numbers to shared memory, and after one barrier every thread adds the four warps in their order, for all the sums of the kernel at once; (2) with at most 512 rows the first kernel has one block, which writes the results, and the host launches the second only for more parts; (3) a loop over tuples that may share particles and has sums launches min(tuples, particles) rows, each thread taking every so manyth tuple (D103), and reduces those rows; (4) `md-exec-accumulate-destinations` merges two destinations of one loop over tuples that the chain of sums of a loop over particles adds one after the other: the kernel yields the sum of the two contributions of each member, so the forces of a term over centers are one field, which accumulates onto the terms before it and is evaluated once per tuple with atomics (D84); (5) `md-exec-fuse-loops` fuses two loops over the tuples of one set when the ops between them that use the first are pure and the second uses none of them, moving those ops after the second, so that the virials of the sums, each weighted by a number computed between them, are one loop at the steps that write energies. The order of each sum depends only on the numbers of rows and parts, so the sums stay deterministic; they are not those of before to the bit. The bond now takes 0.24 ms a step (2.41 s; 2.15 without it), 202.9 µs of kernels against 250.6. A dihedral between the centers of residues 1 to 5, 20 to 30, 60 to 70, and 150 to 159, 21 sums, did not compile: the default list scheduler of LLVM (`ScheduleDAGRRList`), whose test of the chains of calls follows every path through the token factors of a block, stalled for more than ten minutes on a block of the host code with 600 calls and 190 kernels, among them the virials of the 21 sums at the steps that write energies, each a loop and two reduction kernels. With those loops fused it still did, on a block of 95 calls among 1,400 stores of the arguments of kernels; `mdir run` now chooses the fast scheduler (`-pre-RA-sched=fast`) for the host code alone, after the kernels were compiled with the default, unless the command line chose one. The dihedral compiles in 15 s and takes 0.26 ms a step (2.57 s); JAC without a term compiles in 10 s either way and runs at the same rate. Not kept: a packed buffer of the sums, which the one pass did not need; the runs of D145 still bound the parameters.
