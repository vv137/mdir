# D139

Recorded 2026-10-03.

**A term over tuples may take the centers of groups as its members: [[energy.bond]], [[energy.angle]], and [[energy.dihedral]] with `groups`, masks of Amber, in place of `particles`, the custom centroid bond force of OpenMM and a restraint between pull groups.** `groups` gives one mask for each member of the one tuple of the term, and `weighting` weighs the particles of a center by their masses (`"MASS"`, the default) or alike (`"NONE"`). A bond takes, besides `r`, the components `dx`, `dy`, `dz` of the vector from the first center to the second, in Å, so that a pull along one direction or in some dimensions is written by its expression; an angle and a dihedral take `theta` as the terms over particles do. The parameters are numbers. A center is the weighted mean of its particles in the minimum image from a reference, the particle in the middle of the group by number, and the centers are taken from the reference of the first group, so that a group that lies across a face of the cell has its center while every particle lies within half the cell of its reference along each edge; the coordinates of the input must show each within 0.45 of the cell, a tenth to spare, and a run refuses a group that does not, or that selects no particle. In the IR a term has one tuple set: the pairs of a member and the reference of its group, and for each later group the pair of its reference and that of the first, with a field for each group and each such link that holds the weight of the member in its pairs, 1 in the pair of the link, and 0 elsewhere; three `md.sum_tuples` for each field, of the components of the weighted displacements, `displacement(0, 1)`, give the centers, and the coordinate and the expression are numbers outside the loops. The forces and the virial follow from the derivatives of the energy with respect to the sums (D2): differentiation takes a displacement through its components, one unit vector at a time (ops.md, Section 5.4), and the weights of the loops of forces are numbers computed from the sums. `md-exec-fuse-loops` fuses loops over the tuples of one set, so that the sums of a term are one loop and their forces another. The energy is that of the input in mixed precision as well; a term over centers cannot yet call a tabulated function, whose lookup is lowered in loops only. On the dipeptide (`test/Driver/centroid-terms.test`, and on a GPU): a bond in `r` and `dz` between the centers of mass of the peptide and of a water, an angle between the centers of geometry of its three residues, and a dihedral with the fifth, 247.012569, 2.234300, and 1.161051 kcal/mol, as `check_centroid.py` computes them; the forces on every particle of a group agree with central differences within $10^{-6}$ of the largest, and the diagonal of the virial with the sum of $x \otimes F$ over the images that each term takes within $10^{-9}$; with the coordinates moved by half the box and folded into it, the peptide across three faces, the same energies. **Measured** (RTX 3090 at 300 W, JAC, 10,000 steps, mixed precision, GPU 0, three runs each): 0.21 ms a step without a term and 0.28 ms with a bond between the centers of residues 1 to 5 and 150 to 159 (0.29 with a set for each group and link, before they were one). The profile (nsys, 2,000 steps) puts the 60 µs in the kernels, 197 µs a step against 256: the two kernels that reduce the nine sums of the loop take 20 µs, since each sum is a tree of its own over a block; the loops of the sums and of the forces run a group of threads for every particle of the system, as the loops of the bonds do, although some 170 particles belong to the set. The host reads the sums at each step to compute the weights, but it waits at each step for the test of the neighbor structures as well: computing the weights in each kernel from the sums on the device gained 3% and was not kept.
