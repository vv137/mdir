# D244

Recorded 2026-10-10.

**A Python simulation runs at constant pressure in a triclinic cell: the tilts that the barostat scales with the cell (D127) and reports at each scaling become the tilts of the state of the host at the end of a part, through which `State.cell`, a checkpoint, the frames of the reporters, `Borrow.tilt`, and the next activation (D238) have them; a part that fails restores the cell of before with its tilts.** Issue #254, PR #255; lifts the refusal that D238 left. The program, its kernels, and the path of a step are unchanged: the tilts are start values of the entry (D227), which `startActivation` takes from the state of the host since D238, so what remained was the way back, from the runtime (`mdrtSetTilt` writes the tilts into the checkpoint of the output and the open trajectory) to the host. A simulation now gives the checkpoint of the output the cell of the host when it is built and when it continues a checkpoint, takes the tilts from it after each part that ran under a barostat in a triclinic cell, and restores the cell after a failed part with `setCell`, the one place where D238 sets the cell of the host, as a commit that is undone does (the frames of an open reporter then have the cell of before as well). No control-file key, no key of the Python model, and no file format change. *What a Python simulation does not take*: the Python model has the isotropic coupling alone (`mdir.Ensemble` has no key for `coupling`), so the semi-isotropic and anisotropic barostats that `mdir run` takes in a triclinic cell are not programs of a Python simulation, and nothing is refused; it has the neighbor matrix (#270), which holds every image within its reach under a barostat (D241), so the stop of the groups below their reach (D242) is not reached from Python. *Evidence* (403 waters in the rhombic dodecahedron of `test/Driver/Inputs/triclinic`, deterministic, CPU and an RTX 3090, double and mixed; `python-triclinic-npt*.test`, `python-triclinic-reach*.test`): with rigid water, PME, velocity Verlet at 2 fs, and stochastic velocity and cell rescaling every 10 steps, 200 steps of a Python simulation in one part, in parts of 10, and in parts of 30, 70, and 100 give the positions, the velocities, the forces, the diagonal, and the tilts of `mdir run` to the bit, and its energy file and DCD trajectory byte for byte, while the barostat moves the tilts by $2.0\times10^{-2}$ nm and keeps $c_x/a_x = c_y/b_y = 1/2$ exactly; the two agree to the bit when their checkpoints are at the same steps, as for an orthorhombic cell, since a checkpoint ends a segment and the neighbor structures are built anew after it (a run with a checkpoint at step 100 is $5\times10^{-11}$ nm from one without at step 200); a checkpoint at step 100 written by `mdir run` and continued by Python, written by Python (with the tilts of the checkpoint of `mdir run`, to the bit) and continued by `mdir run --continue`, by a second simulation, and by the one that wrote it, each end in the state and the files of `mdir run`; the cell of each of 20 frames is that of the state of its step in DCD ($7\times10^{-16}$ nm), in XTC ($10^{-7}$ nm, f32), and in H5MD (`box/edges`, to the bit); with flexible water, a commit of tilts followed by 40 steps under the barostat equals a simulation compiled from the committed state to the bit, a frame of the run committed into the simulation that ran it evaluates as a simulation compiled from it, and `Borrow.tilt` reads the tilts that the barostat left; 20 frames of a Python run give the frame evaluator (D240) and the commits (D238) the energies, derivatives, virials, volumes, and forces of simulations compiled from them, to the bit; and in the narrow tilted cell of D241, which the barostat takes from 2.2 to 1.92 nm with a pairlist distance of 1.1 nm, the potential of each of 79 rows of a Python run equals the sum over every image to $10^{-5}$ kcal/mol.
