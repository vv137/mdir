# D181

Recorded 2026-10-04.

**On a device, a reduction of one part computes the contributions of its rows in its own block, and adjacent loops that evaluate each tuple once without global sums are launched as one kernel; a term over the centers of groups then costs two kernels fewer.** Measured on JAC (RTX 3090, mixed, groups, a harmonic bias on the distance between the centers of two groups of 20 backbone atoms, `~/notes/mdir/plumed-bench`): the bias cost three single-block kernels a step, 12.5 µs, against 8.6 µs for OpenMM 8.2's `CustomCentroidBondForce`. The kernels were the contributions of the 40 rows (2.3 µs), the reduction of one part that adds them (4.0 µs), and the forces (5.2 µs). The weights $m_i/M_g$ were already a field computed once (#20 supposed otherwise); the cost was the launches. (1) When a loop evaluates each tuple once and its sums have one part (at most 512 rows) and one run of descriptors, the kernel of the reduction computes the contributions of row $i$ where it would load them, in the same order, and the kernel that stores them is launched only for several parts; the sums are bitwise those of D150. (2) Consecutive loops over tuples that evaluate each tuple once (D84, D103) and have no global sums, take the same positions and cell, and read nothing that another adds to are launched as one kernel over their tuples one after the other, each thread finding its loop by the running totals of the tuples; constants and arithmetic on the host may lie between them, and the kernel is launched at the last loop. They add to their destinations with atomics as before, so the deterministic mode, which does not evaluate tuples once, is unchanged. JAC: without the bias the five bonded kernels become one, 0.2308 → 0.2223 ms/step (−3.7%); with it, 0.2312 ms/step, +8.7 and +9.1 µs over the run without it (two runs of 50,000 steps), against +8.4 and +8.8 µs for OpenMM. Amends D150, whose second kernel of one block the first replaces, and D156.
