# D[async-output]

Recorded at its merge.

**The frames of a trajectory of a run of dynamics are copied from the device and written while the steps go on: the call of a frame takes the positions and the numbers of the particles in place and stages copies of them in a slot of a ring of three, on a GPU by a copy within the device on the stream of the kernels and a copy from there to pinned memory of the host on a stream of copies of its own, and one thread writes the frames from the slots in the order of the steps. Every frame queued is written before a checkpoint stages its state, before the trajectory closes, at a stop on a signal or the wall time, before a failure ends the run, at the end of each part of a Python simulation, and at the exit of the process; the files are those of a run that writes each frame in its call, byte for byte.** #113. The dynamics entries (`SimpleStepEntryIR.cpp`) call `mdrtStageFrame` with the fields `in_place` (D215) where they called `mdrtWriteFrame`, which a minimization keeps. On a GPU the call copies each buffer into a buffer of the device that the slot owns, ordered after the step that produced it and before any later kernel overwrites it, records an event, and the stream of copies waits for it and copies to pinned memory (`mdrtStaging*` in `runtime/mdrt_cuda.c`, [runtime.md](../runtime.md), Section 5.3); on the CPU the call copies the buffers of the host. The thread waits for the slot's second event, orders, converts, and writes the frame as `mdrtWriteFrame` does (the order of the input; Å in f32 for DCD and XTC, with XTC's compression; the state in nm for H5MD, under the mutex of #262), and gives the slot back. A call that finds the three slots taken waits for the thread, so the memory stays bounded: three copies of the positions and the numbers of the particles on the device and in pinned memory, 0.66 MB each on JAC and 30 MB on STMV. The changes of the cell of the trajectory that a barostat makes (`mdrtSetBox`, `mdrtSetTilt`) go through the same queue, so each frame has the cell it had. Without a periodic cell the call waits for its own copies and checks the spread of the particles (D142) before it queues the write, so the run stops at the same step with the same error. A write that fails on the thread (H5MD) drops the works queued after it, and the next frame, checkpoint, stop, failure, or end reports its error with the status 1, as the call did; rows of the log of the steps in between may come before the stop (with the writes held back by 100 ms and the frame of step 100 failing, the rows of steps 110 to 140 are printed and the call of the frame of step 140 stops the run). A kernel that fails while frames are queued: once a kernel has failed, the driver returns the error of the context for every event, also for one whose copies had completed before (measured on an RTX 4090: `cuEventQuery` and `cuEventSynchronize` returned `CUDA_ERROR_LAUNCH_FAILED` for an event of a stream synchronized before the failing launch), so `mdrtStagingEnd` also has the stream of copies run a function of the host that counts the slot's copies as arrived, and `mdrtStagingWait` returns at once for a slot whose arrivals equal its stagings (a count, since the function of a staging may run after its event has been waited for and the slot staged again). A failure of the driver ends the process with `abort`, so the runtime first calls a hook of the host (`mdrtSetFailureHook`), with which the thread writes the frames whose copies had arrived and drops the others; an error of the device that the thread meets is handed to the runtime on the thread of the steps (`mdrtStagingFail`), which reports it as any failure of the driver. The run ends with the runtime's report, once, and the frames before the failure are in the file, as when the call wrote them. Without the staging functions of the runtime (a defect), the call stops the run and, in an embedding program (D196), gives its slot back and returns. `stopOnFailure` writes the frames queued before a failure and reports the earlier error of a frame first. A Python simulation waits for its frames at the end of each part (`mdrtPartBoundary`), before its reporters change, before a cell set from Python or a checkpoint, and when it ends; a frame written from the state (H5MD with velocities or forces, D239) has no frames queued before it, since such a trajectory has no staged frames and a change of the reporters waits for those of the trajectory before. `MDIR_FRAME_WRITE_DELAY` holds each write back for a test, `MDIR_FRAME_WRITE_FAIL` fails the write of one frame to H5MD, `MDRT_FAULT_AFTER_FRAMES` makes a kernel trap after the copies of the n-th frame, `MDRT_STAGING_MARK_DELAY` holds back the count of arrivals, and `MDIR_CHECKPOINT_TIMES` times the call (the wait for a slot and the staging) and the write ([environment.md](../environment.md)). No control-file key, file format, default, or overwrite behavior changes; the ring of three is not a key. *Measured* (JAC, 23,558 atoms, a box of TIP3P water of 12,600 atoms, and Cellulose, 408,609 atoms, on RTX 4090s, mixed precision, NVE velocity Verlet of 2 fs, groups; runs of 100,000 steps with a rate every 1000, the mean of the 100 windows after the first 10, which moves by at most 0.61% when 25% or 50% are dropped instead; four jobs on four cards, two for JAC and the water box and two for Cellulose, each running `main` 69d7cafd and the change alternated twice; the ratios are the change against `main` in each job): with energies and frames every 10 steps, JAC with DCD ran at 0.1951 against 0.1633 ms/step (ratios 0.835 and 0.839) and with XTC at 0.2388 against 0.1631 (0.681 and 0.685), against 0.1615 with the energies alone on both (the frames' share from +20.8% and +47.9% to +1.1% and +1.0%); the water box at 0.1369 against 0.1187 (0.866 and 0.868) and 0.1592 against 0.1186 (0.744 and 0.747), against 0.1170 and 0.1171 (+17.0% and +36.1% to +1.4% and +1.3%).
