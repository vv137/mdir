# D207

Recorded 2026-10-06.

**A Python simulation's reporters write `mdir run`'s energy file and trajectory inside the parts of a run, at times that are arguments of the compiled program, and call Python functions at their steps.** M2a item 4 (#109); the maintainer asked for the design that performs best. `Simulation.reporters` takes `EnergyReporter(file, period)` (the columns file of D149), `TrajectoryReporter(file, period, format=None)` (DCD or XTC), at most one of each, and `CallbackReporter(function, period)`, called as `function(simulation, state)` with the GIL held; a report is due where the step is a multiple of its period (OpenMM's model; the energy file of a reporter present at the first run also holds step 0, as `mdir run`'s does). Files are backed up as `mdir run` backs up its outputs, appended across runs, and closed by `close_reporters()` or the simulation's end. The program of segments (D196) takes the count of its report intervals, the periods of coupling of each, and the frame period as arguments: its second nest runs K intervals of R steps, R the greatest common divisor of the built-in periods (a multiple of the coupling period, or else each report ends a part), each ending with the step of energy that `mdir run` takes at a row of its log; the host decides which row is written, and a frame is copied only where the frame period is due. Rows and frames go through `mdir run`'s own host functions and writers, so asynchronous writers (#113) can follow on that shared path for both front ends. A callback ends a part at its step. With the barostat of Trotter type a built-in period must be a multiple of the coupling period. *Evidence.* On the dipeptide in water, deterministic: the energy file and the DCD and XTC trajectories of 100 steps equal those of `mdir run` byte for byte in NVE (CPU and GPU, mixed and double) and in NVT in double; in NVT in mixed precision within three times the difference of `mdir run` in mixed and double (#105); with and without reporters the state is equal to the bit (NVT, cutoff, no constraints). On JAC on one RTX 3090, energies every 100 steps cost 1.1%, every 1000 steps 0.8%, DCD every 1000 steps 1.2%, and a Python callback every 1000 steps 0.8% (with #110's fix; +46% without it).
