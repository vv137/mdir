# D225

Recorded 2026-10-07.

**A part of a Python simulation that fails runs to its end without any access outside a buffer, whatever its positions or cell, and a barostat that scales an edge of the cell by a factor outside [1/2, 2] at one coupling, or makes it not finite, fails the run.** Issue #168; the maintainer's decisions on PR #182. Amends D107 and D196. A build of a neighbor matrix on a device gives a position that is not a number or is beyond $10^{100}$ no place and leaves $-1$ in the order of the cells (D107); `mdir run` stops there, but a Python simulation records the failure and runs the part to its end (D196), and the loops over the rows took particle $-1$ and read before the buffers of the particles (compute-sanitizer: `pair_for` 24 bytes before the positions of 1168 particles in f64). The kernel of the rows (`launchRows`) now takes a place left empty as not valid, and the gather into the order of the cells takes particle 0 there; the structure of groups already padded its places. A barostat whose pressure had blown up scaled the cell from 1.7 nm to about $10^{11}$ nm, which the check of twice the cutoff let through, and the builds asked for about $10^{11}$ cells along each axis: an allocation of $6.6 \times 10^{18}$ bytes ended the process on a device, and a Python run on the CPU crashed the same way, while `mdir run` on the CPU went on with energies of $10^{11}$ kcal/mol. `mdrtSetBox`, shared by both front ends, fails the run on an edge that is not finite or that one coupling scaled by a factor outside [1/2, 2] (a normal coupling scales by about $1 \pm 10^{-4}$). The number of cells along each axis of every build (matrix on the host and the device, groups, triclinic) is at least 1 (3 for the candidates of the groups) and at most 256, computed with `maxnumf` and `minnumf`, which take the other operand for one that is not a number; wider cells cost only time, since the matrix derives its range of search from the count and the groups clamp the range of candidates to the whole grid. The largest system of the Amber suite takes at most 77 (Cellulose, matrix). The points of PME take 0 for a coordinate that is not a number before the conversion to an integer. The bounds of "far outside the cell" stay those of D107 ($10^{100}$, matrix) and D176 (a wrap that leaves the cell, groups); the stop says "a position is not a number or is far outside the cell" (maintainer). Not chosen: ending the activation at the failure instead of running the part to its end (a possible follow-up). *Evidence.* On the dipeptide with PME, a restraint of $10^{300}$ kJ/mol/nm² on one atom, two particles on top of one another, and a barostat at $5 \times 10^4$ bar with a cutoff of 0.4 nm raise `SimulationError` in double and mixed precision on the CPU and a GPU, with the matrix and with groups, keep the state from before the part, and leave the device to another simulation, with 0 errors under memcheck; `mdir run` stops at the barostat with a word. A deterministic NPT run of 200 steps in parts equals main to the bit (CPU and GPU, double and mixed). JAC in mixed precision on one RTX 3090: 0.205 against 0.204 ms per step with groups, 0.274 against 0.275 with the matrix.
