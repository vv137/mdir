# D[reduction-partials]

Recorded at its merge.

**On a device, the second buffer of each global sum in `scratch`, which takes the partial sums of the parts of the reduction, has 1024 rows (`md_exec::maxReductionParts`) and is allocated once in the body of the function for each buffer of contributions, instead of a row a particle from the pool of its region.** Part of #711. `md-exec-assign-storage` gave each sum two buffers of a row a particle (`getScratch`), but the reduction of D150 writes the partial sums of at most `block-size` parts (128 by default) and reads no more, so all but the first rows of the second buffer were never written. At 196,608 particles of TIP3P that buffer held 13.5 MiB for the virial alone (nine f64 a row) and 1.5 MiB for each f64 sum. *Why one for each buffer of contributions*: the contributions still come from the pool of the region and are shared among the sums as before (a loop around them carries them), and a buffer of partial sums is shared exactly where its contributions are, so `md-exec-assign-streams` sees the same dependencies between the sums and orders them as before; a buffer of partial sums is never put in the pool (`Scope::fixedLength`), since its type is that of buffers with a row a particle and the pool reuses buffers by type and set (D337). The ops' verifier is unchanged: the types are those of the buffers of a set. `convert-md-exec-to-gpu` refuses a `block-size` above 1024 (the device's limit on the threads of a block), so no reduction has more parts than the rows. The CPU has no scratch and is unchanged. *Alternatives*: sizing the buffer by `block-size` would tie `md-exec-assign-storage` to an option of a later pass, for 1024 × 72 bytes at most; one shared buffer of partial sums for the whole function would add dependencies between sums on different streams. *Validation* (one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, `main` 66e1164d and the branch built the same way, the water boxes of #710): the rows of 4 steps of NVE from the same checkpoint in double precision are identical to the last digit for TIP3P (196,608 atoms) and OPC (262,144 particles); in mixed precision they differ by at most 2e-8 of the total energy, where the order of the atomic additions of the groups is not fixed (neighbor-groups.md) and the reductions read the same rows in the same order. At the peak in use at 196,608 TIP3P atoms the blocks of a row a particle that go are exactly partial sums: one of nine f64 (14,155,776 bytes), one of three (4,718,592), one of two (3,145,728), and one f64 (1,572,864), while the blocks under 1 MiB grow by 0.3 MiB; no new block of a row a particle appears (OPC alike). `mdir emit --stage=lowered` of the CPU program is byte-identical to `main`'s for TIP3P at 3,072 atoms in mixed and double precision. compute-sanitizer's memcheck finds no error in 4 steps at 3,072 atoms (the matrix) and at 24,576 (groups). 28 GPU tests of the driver and the integration tests that sum energies, the virial, PME, barostats, and groups pass. *Measured* (`MDIR_DEVICE_MEMORY=2`, the driver's use at the peak with the pool, MiB, `main` → branch): TIP3P 3,072 atoms 581.2 → 581.2, 10,368 603.2 → 603.2, 24,576 615.2 → 613.2, 82,944 713.2 → 703.2, 196,608 925.2 → 901.2; OPC 3,072 587.2 → 587.2, 10,368 625.2 → 625.2, 24,576 637.2 → 631.2, 82,944 799.2 → 785.2, 196,608 1,059.2 → 1,029.2. The growth from 3,072 to 196,608 atoms goes from 344 to 320 MiB for TIP3P and from 472 to 442 for OPC. The rates are unchanged: NVE, 12,000 steps timed, 3 rounds alternated, median 408.1 → 408.5 ns/day for TIP3P at 196,608 atoms and 273.4 → 272.9 for OPC.
