# D213

Recorded 2026-10-07.

**A Python model declares tunable parameters, whose values a simulation takes without compiling: each a named vector $\boldsymbol\theta$ with a map $\iota$ from its sites (particles for `"charge"`, Lennard-Jones types for `"sigma"` and `"epsilon"` with the combining rule, one site for a constant of a pair term, tuples for a parameter of a tuple term), and an update puts the values into the model and rebuilds the program's values by the code that compiles them, refusing any value that would change the program's text.** Issue #130, the M2a gate left open by D192 and the base of M2b (D195). `System.tunables` (`mdir.Tunable(name, parameter, term, map, values, mixing)`) is a compile input, so declaring is structural; `Simulation.tunables` is a mapping of names to $(M,)$ arrays, the shape of their gradients, with atomic `update`, a value version, `history` of `(step, version)`, `State.tunables_version`, and a last column `tunables_version` in the energy file of a simulation with tunables. Nothing that depends on a tunable is a constant of the text: charges, the type table, and tuple fields are entry buffers already; a tunable constant of a pair term is read from a $1\times K$ table; with a barostat, the virial and energy of the constant terms are arguments of the entry. The derived quantities (the 1-4 products, the self term and background of PME, the reaction field's self term, $\langle C_6\rangle$ and its shift estimate, the tails of pair terms with their classes of particles) are recomputed on the host; their derivatives belong to the M2b evaluator (question to the maintainer on PR #159). Per-type $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$ rebuild the table by Lorentz (or geometric) and Berthelot rules from the types' own values; a pair of types that the rule does not reproduce within $10^{-6}$ (NBFIX) keeps its values, and compile warns once listing such pairs (maintainer's decision on PR #159); Amber's tables follow it within $2\times10^{-7}$; the 1-4 pairs keep their own parameters. After an update during a run the entry is called with no steps and `%first_call = 2`, which evaluates the forces of the state anew without leapfrog's half kick back, so that the next step takes the forces of the new Hamiltonian; `run(0, energy=True)` makes the same evaluation. *Evidence.* An update at step 8 of velocity Verlet NVE (charges, $\sigma$, $\epsilon$, a pair constant, tuple constants), then 12 steps, equals a compile with the new values from that state to the bit on the CPU and a GPU, double and mixed (deterministic); so does NPT updated before its first run; against OpenMM 8.6.1 and NumPy the energy at new values is within $1.3\times10^{-3}$ kJ/mol and its change within $8.6\times10^{-4}$ of $253.0$; the dispersion correction matches its formula to $10^{-14}$; central differences in a pair constant match `observe` (D189) to $4.7\times10^{-8}$; on propane and water with an NBFIX of CT and OW an update equals a compile with the rule's table but for CT-OW to the bit, and its change of the energy a NumPy sum that keeps CT-OW to $1.5\times10^{-11}$ kJ/mol. Without tunables the module and lowered IR equal main's on the Amber suite and the rates are main's; tunable charges and per-type Lennard-Jones cost nothing measurable on JAC and Factor IX; an update takes 10 ms on JAC (20 ms with the evaluation). See [python-tunable.md](../python-tunable.md). *Amended 2026-10-07 (#184, maintainer's decision on PR #183):* maps are built from `System.topology` (D221), the one place of the topology data; the read-only per-particle arrays `System.charges`, `particle_types`, `type_names`, `atom_names`, `residue_indices`, and `residue_names` were removed before any release. *Amended by D230 (maintainer's decision on PR #205, Q3):* the derivatives of the derived quantities are not computed by an IR of the evaluator from $\boldsymbol\theta$; the program gives the derivative of the energy in each of its buffers and the host applies the chain rule through `applyTunables`, with closed forms and the quadratures for the constants of the energy.
