# D263

Recorded 2026-10-16.

**Stochastic velocity rescaling and the Nosé–Hoover chain control the optimal temperature of Jung, Kobayashi, and Sugita (2019) [[Jung2019]](../references.md#jung2019), $N_fk_BT_\text{opt} = \tfrac43K_\text{half} + \tfrac23K$, not the kinetic energy $K$ of the velocities they scale.** Issue #268, amends D45 and D203. The factor of the thermostat is that of $K_\text{opt} = K + \tfrac23(K_\text{half} - K)$; it scales every velocity, so the constraints of the velocities hold, and $N_f$ is unchanged ($3N - N_c$, less 3 when the motion of the center of mass is removed). $K_\text{half}$ is exact from the forces without constraints, $K + \tfrac{\Delta t^2}{8}\sum_i\lVert\mathbf F_i\rVert^2/m_i$ [[Jung2018]](../references.md#jung2018); with constraints it is measured as D203 measures it for the rows of the log (a kick, a drift, and the constraints of the positions of the next step, the chord velocities), now in every step that closes a period of coupling as well. Both thermostats count into the bath the change of $K$ that their scaling makes, exactly, so the conserved energy of the log is the effective energy of [[Bussi2007]](../references.md#bussi2007) for either (for the chain it was the change of the chain's energy, which is no longer conserved when the chain is driven by $K_\text{opt}$). *Evidence (#268):* with SETTLE at 2 fs the state, from $\langle U\rangle$ at a fixed volume against a run at 0.5 fs, was +1.7 to +1.8 K above the target when the thermostat held $K$ and +0.3 to +0.5 K when it held $K_\text{opt}$ (+7.3 and +2.0 K at 4 fs); the canonical distribution of the controlled $K_\text{opt}$ was not rejected.
