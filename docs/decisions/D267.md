# D267

Recorded 2026-10-16.

**The CPU target closes its gap to GROMACS in five steps, in this order, each its own PR: (1) LLVM's module pipeline on the host code, at O2 or O3 as the rate and the host compile time measured in a job decide, the level in the key of the compile cache; (2) PME on the host in parallel, the spreading by atomic adds to the fixed-point grid, so that a result is the same to the bit for any number of threads (D70), f32 in the mixed mode where the mixed mode of a device takes f32, and the energy and virial of the convolution only where a step writes them; (3) the builds of the list, whose structure on the CPU is decided once step 4 is measured; (4) the loop over pairs vectorized in the compiler's lowering, not in hand-written intrinsics, which supersedes the plan of #33 for its kernels (its MPI part stays on hold for M4); (5) the rest of the step.** Issue #359; the maintainer's rulings on PR #361 (2026-10-16). *Measured* (`scripts/benchmarks/cpu-breakdown/`; EPYC 9354, 16 cores of a Slurm job; mixed precision; TIP3P boxes of 13,899 to 309,876 sites and JAC; alternated rounds, the first dropped; GROMACS 2026.3 `-nb cpu -pme cpu`, AVX2_256): MDIR is 15.9 to 22.4 times slower than GROMACS with its default list policy and 10.8 to 13.8 with an exact buffer (cutoff + 3 Å, nstlist 10); on the box of 309,876 sites, 299 against 15.5 ms. Both engines are linear in the size, with no part that grows faster: MDIR 947 ns a site and step, GROMACS 47. *By cause, on that box at 16 threads* (a sampler of the JIT code, the share of each part of the main thread's wall clock): PME 156 ms (spreading 64, FFT 54, convolution 34, all serial and in f64, and gathering 4.4) against 7.2; the loop over pairs 93 against 6.7; the builds of the matrix 37, one every 5.6 steps, against 0.7 (search and domain decomposition); the rest 13 against 0.9. On one thread MDIR is 15.6 times slower (2456 against 157 ms) and the threads make the 19: MDIR gains 8.3 on 16, GROMACS 10.3. The list policy is not the cause: the matrix's 147.5 M entries a step (reach 10.5 Å, each pair twice) are 1.10 times the 134 M atom pairs that GROMACS's 4×8 clusters compute (each pair once, padding and pairs beyond the cutoff included). What differs is the cost of a pair: 10.1 ns of one thread an entry against 0.80 ns a pair, because the loop is scalar f32 (63 instructions an entry, a division, the Lennard-Jones coefficients read in f64), with no vectorization across pairs, while GROMACS's kernel computes 8 pairs at once. *The host code* is compiled by the code generator alone: no inlining (the helpers of the templates, such as `mdrt.minimum_image` three times for each candidate of a search, are calls), no vectorizer. LLVM's O3 pipeline, in a commit that only timed it, took 299.8 to 219.3 ms on the large box and 21.3 to 15.1 on JAC (EPYC 7543), with the same energies to every printed digit.
