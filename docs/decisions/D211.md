# D211

Recorded 2026-10-06.

**A Python simulation compiles one program of segments, whose entry does the work of the start only on its first call, and every lowering of the process takes MLIR's threads from one pool of the process.** Issue #147; the maintainer's decision on PR #150. Amends D196, under which a simulation compiled a program for the first segment at its creation and, on its first part after it, a second program that continued the state, a full lowering and JIT of a nearly identical program on the path of the run, and D199, whose simulation then owned two engines. The entry of a program of segments takes `%first_call`. On the first call it does the work of the start: for dynamics, the forces, energy, and virial at the start, the row of step 0 with its terms, kinetic energy, and constraint trace, the outputs of pulls, free energy, and observables at the start, and the half kick back of leapfrog; for a minimization (D202), the projection of the positions onto the constraints and the terms. On a later call it takes the forces and velocities given (the positions, for a minimization), as the continuation program did. The signature is the union of the two, and the first call of dynamics is given zero forces that it does not read. The branch is a loop of one iteration on the first call and none on the others, carrying the values given, the idiom of the optional step of energy of D196: the passes that place fields and their buffers (`md-exec-assign-precision`, `md-exec-assign-storage`, the neighbor and kernel passes) know the loops that carry fields, and the first two refused a field-valued `scf.if`. A barostat that scales the cell every step, which a simulation refuses already, is refused by the builder for a program of segments, since the state that its first scaling takes is not an argument. `Control::continuesSegment` is removed. Not chosen: two entries sharing the loop of steps, since `md-inline` inlines that loop into each and calls would cut optimizations between the start and the first step; and compiling the second program on a thread of its own while the first compiles, the first design of this PR, which hid the second compile but kept its CPU time. Each MLIR context of a lowering (the simulation's, `mdir.compile`'s) is created without threads and given one `llvm::DefaultThreadPool` through `MLIRContext::setThreadPool`, never destroyed; a forked child makes its own. `mdir run` and `--continue` keep their two programs (#153). *Evidence.* In the deterministic mode, the Python ala3 example (`--steps-scale 0.002`: minimization, NVT, NPT, and 1,000 production steps over parts) gives the final state, the energy files of the three dynamics stages, the DCD, and the minimized positions of main to the bit, on the CPU and a GPU, in mixed and double precision. `python-one-program-gpu.test` finds no module of kernels loaded after a simulation's creation through runs and minimizations of three calls, where main loaded 139 and 88. The example at `--steps-scale 0.01` on GPU 0 (RTX 3090, 300 W), with the driver's cache of compiled PTX warm: mixed 240.0 s on main and 145.9 s here, CPU time 268 s and 220 s; double 250.9 s and 162.2 s, 279 s and 222 s; the first run of the new program in double, with the cache cold, 236.3 s. The runs of stages 2 to 4, which compiled the second program, take 0.06–1.3 s in mixed instead of 20–34 s; `Simulation(program)` takes about as long as main's first compile (7.5–33.6 s against 7.0–36.7 s). The Python GPU tests (`--param gpu_workers=12`, 22 files): 276 s on main and 337 and 211 s here in two runs, while the sum of the times of the files fell from 2,845 to 2,388 and 2,128 s (the three lifetime tests from about 260 s each to about 158 s); the wall time of one run follows the load of the machine. Follow-ups: `mdir.compile` lowers a program that the simulation does not use (#151); the builder's process-wide counter (#152). See [python-segments.md](../python-segments.md#compiles).
