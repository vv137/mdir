# D141

Recorded 2026-10-03.

**A trajectory is in DCD or in the XTC of GROMACS, by the extension of its name (`trajectory_format = "AUTO"`, the default) or as `trajectory_format` says.** XTC holds, for each frame, the step, the time in ps, the vectors of the cell and the positions in nm, the positions rounded to a thousandth of a nm and compressed by the scheme of GROMACS, which MDIR writes on its own (`lib/Driver/Trajectory.cpp`): integers within the bounds of the frame, written with the bits of the product of the three ranges, and runs of particles close to the one before written as small differences, in a size that grows or shrinks by a step of a table of sizes about $2^{1/3}$ apart. A continued run (D130) finds its frames by their headers, cuts the file after those that the checkpoint counts, and appends; `--no-append` writes `<trajectory>.partNNNN.xtc`. The writers share an interface, `TrajectoryWriter`. On the dipeptide, 1168 atoms and 5 frames, the file is 20,844 bytes against 70,676 in DCD; `gmx check` and `gmx dump` of GROMACS 2026.3 read it, and its positions are those of the DCD of the same run within 0.0005 nm, half the precision; `gmx trjconv` writes the same file again byte for byte, so the compression is that of GROMACS. `test/Driver/xtc.test` decodes the frames with a reader of its own in Python against the DCD, and continues a run three times to the file of the run that was not interrupted, byte for byte.
