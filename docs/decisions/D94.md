# D94

Recorded 2026-10-01.

**Under `fast_math`, what a pair kernel computes of the distance alone beyond roots and powers is a table in f32.** `md-exec-simplify-distance{radial=true}` groups the terms of a value of the kernel by their factors free of the distance, and a group whose part that depends on it takes an exponential, erfc, or the like becomes those factors times `md_exec.radial` of a function of the module, `(f64) -> f64`: the screened Coulomb force of Ewald summation is $q_i q_j$ times one function of $r^2$, its energy another. `md-exec-expand-radial`, after the precision is assigned, makes it in an f32 kernel a table over $r^2$ from $2^{-10}$ of the cutoff squared to it: the intervals that the bits of $r^2$ as an f32 number name, $2^b$ of them for each power of two, and in each a cubic in $u = r^2 - (\text{its start})$, exact in f32, fitted at four Chebyshev nodes in f64; $b$ is the fewest from 4 to 12 whose cubics are within $3 \times 10^{-8}$ of the function in f64, relative to its largest value on each interval, and the pass fails unless they are within $3 \times 10^{-7}$ in f32, as a kernel evaluates them (17 points an interval). A table is a constant of the module, four f32 numbers an interval, read with one load of 16 bytes; tables that are the same are one. In an f64 kernel the function is inlined. On JAC (cutoff 8 Å, $b = 7$, 1281 intervals, 20 KB a table), the Coulomb energy at the start is −62721.9863 kcal/mol against −62721.9880 in double precision, from −62721.9826; the loop over pairs at a reach of 9 Å takes 65.4 µs, from 73.1, and on Cellulose 1113, from 1201 (RTX 3090). *Amended 2026-10-03:* a function of the distance holds only the ops that the fit evaluates, which are smooth: arithmetic, roots and powers, exp, log, erf, erfc, and the trigonometric and hyperbolic functions and their inverses (`include/mdir/Dialect/MDExec/Transforms/Radial.h`, shared by both passes). Floor, comparisons, selections, minima, and lookups stay in the kernel, where a tabulated function (D138) has them. Up to then any op of arith or math was taken, and the fit evaluated those it did not know as NaN, which passed its test of the error unseen: a pair term in `cos(r)` ran with NaN energies in mixed precision. The pass now fails on a table that is not finite (`test/Driver/radial-smooth.test`).
