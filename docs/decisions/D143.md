# D143

Recorded 2026-10-03.

**A potential may compute fields from the positions and sum them into its energy, and differentiation carries the derivative of the energy back through them: intermediate fields, the first being the Born radii of generalized Born.** In the md dialect such a potential is a gather over pairs, `md.gather_relation` with `exchange(none)` where its kernel is not symmetric, maps over particles, `md.map_particles`, and sums that read the fields, `md.sum_relation` and `md.sum_particles`; the forward path needed no change, on the CPU and on a GPU with both neighbor structures. `md-differentiate` takes the adjoint of each field, $\bar F_i = \partial E/\partial F_i$, from the last field to the first, from the sums and maps that read it, each a gather or a map of the derivative of their kernel with respect to the field, and turns the adjoint of each gather into forces and a virial: a gather whose kernel is that of the field evaluated from both ends of each pair, $\mathbf F_m = -\sum_b (\bar G_m k'(m, b) + \bar G_b k'(b, m))\,\mathbf d_{mb}/r$ (ops.md, Section 5.6). The adjoints are built before the derivatives of the sums, so that the uses of a field are those of the potential. A gather that reads a field, and a kernel that uses the displacement, are not differentiated yet. The cost is four sweeps over the pairs for energy and forces: the integral, the energy, the adjoint of the radii, and the forces through them. `test/Integration/obc.mlir` writes the energy of OBC II for five particles at the md level [[Hawkins1996]](../references.md#hawkins1996), [[Onufriev2004]](../references.md#onufriev2004): the energy agrees with the formulas within $10^{-12}$, the forces with central differences, extrapolated in the step, within $10^{-9}$ of the largest, and the virial with $\sum \mathbf x \otimes \mathbf F$ within $10^{-9}$, at positions where no pair is within 0.03 nm of a branch of the integral, near which differences straddle a kink and disagree by $6\times10^{-7}$ (the first positions had a pair within $2\times10^{-5}$ nm of one); on a GPU the same with the matrix and the groups, and within $10^{-5}$ in mixed precision.
