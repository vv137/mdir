# D368

Recorded 2026-11-04.

**The instance in f32 of the templates of the neighbor matrix is made in IR from the parsed f64 template, not by substituting text before the parse.** This is step 1 of #739, by ruling 19. `instantiateTemplates` took the text of `NeighborsMatrix.mlir` or `NeighborsMatrixGPU.mlir` and wrote every `f64` as `f32`, every `arith.truncf` as `arith.bitcast`, and every name that began with `@mdrt` with `_f32` added. Each substitution was matched as a substring of the text: it relied on `f64` never standing inside a name or a string, on there being no `arith.extf`, and on the template referring to no runtime function named `@mdrt…`. The audit of #739 found all three true today, but none of them is checked.

`instantiateTemplates(ModuleOp, Type)` now works on the parsed template. An `AttrTypeReplacer` replaces f64 with f32 in every type and attribute: values, arguments, results, function types, and the shapes of memrefs and vectors. A `FloatAttr` or `DenseFPElementsAttr` of f64 becomes the f32 value that it rounds to, to nearest with ties to even. A truncation whose operand and result are now both f32 becomes `arith.bitcast`. The symbols that have a body are renamed through `SymbolTable`. Declarations keep their names, the `@mdir.primitive.*` of the device template among them, so `expandPrimitiveCalls` still resolves them by name. The f64 instance is the parsed template as it is. The host and the device lowerings call it after the parse and, on the device, before the primitives are expanded.

The program does not change. MLIR parses a literal of f32 as a double and rounds it to f32, so the IR path rounds each constant as the text path did. `1.0000000596046447753906250000001 : f32` parses to 1.0, not to the f32 above it. This includes the bound `1.0e100` of a position far off the cell, which is +∞ in f32 (#740 changes that separately). The locations of the template differ, since the columns of the text no longer shift, but they reach neither the lowered program nor its PTX. `test/Conversion/MDExecToGPU/neighbors-matrix-f32.mlir` shows the instance on a device: names, types, casts, the +∞ bound, and no `mdir.primitive` left.

The alternative, building the instance with `OpBuilder` and the precision as a parameter, would rewrite the templates and their generator as C++. See [ops.md, Section 7.6](../ops.md#76-neighbor-structures).
