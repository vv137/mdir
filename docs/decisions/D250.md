# D250

Recorded 2026-10-12.

**For a device, the loop over particles that closes a run of loops over disjoint tuples is not fused with the one that opens the next, so that each of the two runs of a leapfrog step under Langevin dynamics with constraints is one integration kernel** (#287). After #286 that step begins as velocity Verlet does: half a kick, the projection of the velocities, half a kick, the drift with the friction and the noise, the constraints of the positions. On JAC it took 0.1482 ms against 0.1408 for velocity Verlet (RTX 4090, mixed precision, ten runs each, the standard deviations 0.0006 and 0.0004), 5.3% more for the same kicks, projection, and constraints. `md-exec-fuse-loops` made one loop of everything between the two runs of loops over tuples: the loop that adds the change of the projection, the second half kick, and the drift. D110 joins a loop over particles, the loops over disjoint tuples that read it, and the loop over particles that reads them into one kernel, and a loop that is the loop after of one run is not the loop before of the next; so the constraints of the positions stayed as before D110, the four loops over tuples as a kernel of their own, which reads and writes its values in memory, and the loop after them as another (15.5 and 9.1 µs a step with a wait after each launch, against 14.6 and 6.7 for the integration kernel and its kernel of the groups across warps). Velocity Verlet does not meet this: the evaluation of the forces lies between its two runs. The pass takes an option, `integration-runs`, which the driver sets for a device: a loop over particles that closes such a run is not fused with a later one that opens one. A run is one whose loops over tuples are over disjoint sets, have no global sums, read the loop before as a field of their members, and have their destinations read by loops over particles alone; the run that the second loop opens must end in loops over particles that open no third run. The conditions are those under which both runs become kernels: the scaling of the groups of the barostat of Trotter type reads the drift as positions alone, the projection of a step with the virial has loops with global sums that read its destinations, and with virtual sites the placement after the constraints is a third run, for which one kernel would only change places with another. Those programs keep their loops, as does the CPU, where the option is not set and the pipeline is the same text. Not chosen: a kernel of two runs in `convert-md-exec-to-gpu` (the loop between them closing one and opening the next), which would save the two launches of the kernels for the groups across warps at most and changes device code; and a change of the order of the step in the driver, which would change the state it leaves. JAC, ten runs each, interleaved with `main`: leapfrog under Langevin dynamics at constant volume 0.1482 to 0.1447 ms a step (1166 to 1194 ns/day, 2.4% with a standard error of 0.1%), with C-rescale every 25 steps 0.1594 to 0.1554 (1084 to 1112 ns/day, 2.5% with a standard error of 0.2%); Cellulose (408,609 atoms), eight runs each, 2.619 to 2.587 ms (66.0 to 66.8 ns/day, 1.2% with a standard error of 0.08%) and with C-rescale 2.659 to 2.635 (0.9%). The 2.7% that remain against velocity Verlet (0.1408) are one launch: the sum of the forces ends a leapfrog step as a kernel of its own, in every leapfrog program, because the kick that reads it begins the next iteration of the loop of steps, while velocity Verlet computes it in the kernel of its second half kick. At constant energy leapfrog is nonetheless faster than velocity Verlet (0.1282 against 0.1319) because it needs no projection, a run of two launches; under Langevin dynamics both need both runs, and leapfrog keeps its launch of the sum. Removing it means fusing loops across iterations of the loop of steps, which is not done here. The lowered programs of the suite's own runs (velocity Verlet and leapfrog, NVE and NPT with velocity rescaling) and of velocity Verlet under Langevin dynamics are the same text as on `main`. In the deterministic mode the state after 40 steps and the rows of the log are identical to those of `main` for 41 programs on a GPU in mixed and in double precision (both integrators; NVE, velocity rescaling, the Nosé–Hoover chain, Langevin dynamics; each work of the barostat; SHAKE with SETTLE, each alone, virtual sites; a minimization). The loops change in two kinds of program: leapfrog under Langevin dynamics with constraints, and velocity Verlet with virtual sites, where the loop that adds the forces spread from the sites closes one run and the kick opens the projection of the velocities, which is now an integration kernel as well (OPC water, 70.9 and 70.8 µs a step with a scatter of 0.5: no change of the rate). In both a plain step launches as many kernels as before, and a step that writes energies or scales the cell one more. `test/Dialect/MDExec/Transforms/fuse-integration-runs.mlir`; `test/Driver/leapfrog-langevin-kernels-gpu.test` (four integration kernels a step against two without the option, and the same state to the bit in double and mixed precision).
